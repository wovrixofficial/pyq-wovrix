Alternating Current

AC Voltage
$$V = V_0 \sin(\omega t + \phi)$$
RMS Value
⭐ High-Yield
$$V_{\text{rms}} = \frac{V_0}{\sqrt{2}} \approx 0.707 V_0$$
AC Power
$$P_{\text{avg}} = V_{\text{rms}} \cdot I_{\text{rms}} \cos\phi$$
Impedance
$$Z = \sqrt{R^2 + (X_L - X_C)^2}$$
Capacitive Reactance
$$X_C = \frac{1}{\omega C} = \frac{1}{2\pi f C}$$
Inductive Reactance
$$X_L = \omega L = 2\pi f L$$
Phase Angle
$$\tan\phi = \frac{X_L - X_C}{R}$$
Resonant Frequency
$$f_r = \frac{1}{2\pi \sqrt{LC}}, \quad \omega_r = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}$$
📌
Topper's Revision Note & Traps:
⭐ Peak vs RMS: Always remember AC ammeters and voltmeters measure RMS values (I_rms = I_0 / √2 ≈ 0.707 I_0), not peak values!

Core Fundamentals & Constants

Coulomb's Law
⭐ High-Yield
$$F = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{|q_1 q_2|}{r^2}$$
Electric Field
$$E = F/q = kQ/r^2 (point charge) - E in N/C. Direction: away from +ve, toward -ve charge$$
Electric Potential
$$V = \frac{kQ}{r}$$
Note: V = W/q (work per unit charge) — V in volts (J/C). Potential energy U = qV = kQq/r
Electric Dipole Moment
$$p = qd (magnitude) - q = charge magnitude, d = separation. Direction from -ve to +ve charge$$
Dipole Potential
$$V = (kp cos \theta )/r^2 = (1/(4\pi \varepsilon_0 ))(\vec{p} \cdot r̂)/r^2 - \theta = angle from dipole axis. V depends on angle and distance$$
Dipole Torque in Field
$$τ = pE sin \theta = \vec{p} \times \vec{E} - τ rotates dipole to align with field$$
Dipole in Uniform Field
$$U = -pE cos \theta = -\vec{p} \cdot \vec{E} - Minimum (stable) when aligned with field$$
📌
Topper's Revision Note & Traps:
⚠️ Common Trap: Always express distance 'r' in meters (not cm or mm), and ensure charges are in Coulombs (convert µC by 10⁻⁶). In a dielectric medium of constant K, F_med = F_air / K.

Solutions, Kinetics & Electrochemistry

Raoult's Law for ideal solutions
⭐ High-Yield
$$P_A = \chi_A \cdot P^\circ_A, \quad P_{\text{total}} = P_A + P_B$$
Relative lowering of vapour pressure
$$\Delta P/P° = χ_solute = n_solute / (n_solute + n_solvent) - Independent of identity of solute (colligative)$$
Note: for non-volatile solutes
Boiling point elevation
$$\Delta Tb = Kb \times m - Kb = molal boiling point elevation constant (°C \cdot kg/mol), m = molality$$
Freezing point depression
$$\Delta Tf = Kf \times m - Kf = molal freezing point depression constant (°C \cdot kg/mol), m = molality$$
Osmotic pressure
$$\pi = i C R T = i \left(\frac{n}{V}\right) R T$$
Note: most direct colligative property
van't Hoff factor
$$i = observed colligative effect / expected effect for 1 mol solute - i ≈ 1 for nonelectrolytes, i > 1 for electrolytes (ions)$$
Note: ΔTb = i·Kb·m
Degree of dissociation (electrolytes)
$$\alpha = (initial moles - final moles) / initial moles - i = 1 + (n-1)\alpha where n = number of ions per formula unit$$
Henry's Law for gas solubility
⭐ High-Yield
$$c = Kₕ \times P_gas - c = concentration (mol/L), Kₕ = Henry's law constant, P in atm$$
Solubility and common ion effect
⭐ High-Yield
$$s = \sqrt{Ksp} (pure water)$$
Note: Ksp = [A⁺]^a × [B⁻]^b for AB — Adding common ion decreases solubility; Ksp independent of concentration
📌
Topper's Revision Note & Traps:
⭐ Ideal Solution: Follows Raoult's Law over entire concentration range. ΔH_mix = 0, ΔV_mix = 0. E.g., Benzene + Toluene, n-hexane + n-heptane.

$$sin⁻¹ x + cos⁻¹ x = \\frac{pi}{2}$$
