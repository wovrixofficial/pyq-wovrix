Chemistry Haloalkanes and Haloarenes 3 Marks
Appeared in: 2024 (2 sets)
Account for the following : (a) The dipole moment of chlorobenzene is lower than that of cyclohexylchloride. (b) Alkyl halides are immiscible in water. (c) t-butyl bromide has lower boiling point than n-butyl bromide.
✓ Verified CBSE Marking Scheme Official Evaluation Steps & EL10 Insight

(a) The dipole moment of chlorobenzene is lower than that of cyclohexyl chloride.

  • Step 1 (Governing Principle): [0.5 Mark] The dipole moment ($\mu$) depends on the hybridization of the carbon atom attached to the chlorine atom and the resonance effect.
  • Step 2 (Step-by-Step Reasoning): [0.5 Mark] In chlorobenzene, the C-atom is $sp^2$ hybridized, which is more electronegative than the $sp^3$ hybridized C-atom in cyclohexyl chloride. Furthermore, the lone pair on the Cl atom participates in resonance with the benzene ring, creating partial double bond character in the $C-Cl$ bond, which reduces the polarity.
  • Step 3 (Final Conclusion): [0.0 Mark] Due to the $sp^2$ hybridization and resonance, the net dipole moment of chlorobenzene is lower than that of cyclohexyl chloride.

💡 EL10 Concept Insight: Think of the benzene ring as a "tug-of-war" partner that pulls some of the chlorine's electron density into itself through resonance, making the bond less "lopsided" (polar) compared to the simple, single-bonded cyclohexyl chloride.

(b) Alkyl halides are immiscible in water.

  • Step 1 (Governing Principle): [0.5 Mark] Solubility is governed by the principle "like dissolves like." For a substance to dissolve in water, the energy released during the formation of new solute-solvent interactions must be sufficient to overcome the energy required to break existing solute-solute and solvent-solvent interactions.
  • Step 2 (Step-by-Step Reasoning): [0.5 Mark] Alkyl halides are polar but cannot form hydrogen bonds with water molecules. The energy required to break the strong hydrogen bonds between water molecules is much greater than the energy released by the weak dipole-dipole interactions formed between alkyl halides and water.
  • Step 3 (Final Conclusion): [0.0 Mark] Since the hydration energy is insufficient to overcome the hydrogen bonding energy of water, alkyl halides remain immiscible.

💡 EL10 Concept Insight: Water molecules are like a tight-knit group of friends holding hands (hydrogen bonds); alkyl halides aren't "cool" enough to join the group, so water pushes them away to stay together.

(c) t-butyl bromide has a lower boiling point than n-butyl bromide.

  • Step 1 (Governing Principle): [0.5 Mark] Boiling point is directly proportional to the magnitude of van der Waals forces, which depend on the surface area of the molecule.
  • Step 2 (Step-by-Step Reasoning): [0.5 Mark] n-butyl bromide is a straight-chain molecule with a larger surface area, leading to stronger intermolecular van der Waals forces. In contrast, t-butyl bromide is branched and spherical in shape, which significantly reduces the surface area available for intermolecular contact.
  • Step 3 (Final Conclusion): [0.0 Mark] The reduced surface area in t-butyl bromide results in weaker van der Waals forces, leading to a lower boiling point compared to n-butyl bromide.

💡 EL10 Concept Insight: Imagine n-butyl bromide as a long piece of Velcro that sticks easily to others, while t-butyl bromide is like a round ball that barely touches anything, making it much easier to pull apart and boil.
