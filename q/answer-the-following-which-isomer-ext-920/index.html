✓ Verified CBSE Marking Scheme Official Evaluation Steps & EL10 Insight

CBSE Class 12 Chemistry: Marking Scheme Solution

(a) Which isomer of $C_4H_9Br$ is most reactive towards $S_N1$ reaction?

  • Step 1 (Governing Principle): [0.5 Mark] The reactivity of alkyl halides towards $S_N1$ reaction depends primarily on the stability of the carbocation intermediate formed during the rate-determining step. The order of stability is: $3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ$.
  • Step 2 (Analysis): [0.25 Mark] Among the isomers of $C_4H_9Br$, tert-butyl bromide ($(CH_3)_3CBr$) forms a tertiary ($3^\circ$) carbocation, which is the most stable due to inductive effect and hyperconjugation.
  • Step 3 (Final Answer): [0.25 Mark] The most reactive isomer is 2-bromo-2-methylpropane (tert-butyl bromide).

(b) Predict the alkene that would be formed by dehydrohalogenation of 1-Bromo-1-methylcyclohexane.

  • Step 1 (Governing Principle): [0.5 Mark] Dehydrohalogenation follows Saytzeff’s rule, where the more substituted alkene (the most stable one) is the major product.
  • Step 2 (Reaction Mechanism): [0.25 Mark] Elimination of $HBr$ from 1-bromo-1-methylcyclohexane can occur at the $C_2$ position (inside the ring) or the methyl group. $$C_7H_{13}Br \xrightarrow{alc. KOH, \Delta} C_7H_{12} + HBr$$
  • Step 3 (Final Answer): [0.25 Mark] The major product is 1-methylcyclohexene.

(c) Although chlorine shows strong $-I$ effect, yet it is ortho- and para-directing in electrophilic aromatic substitution reactions. Why?

  • Step 1 (Governing Principle): [0.5 Mark] The directing influence is determined by the competition between the inductive effect ($-I$) and the resonance effect ($+R$ or $+M$).
  • Step 2 (Mechanism Explanation): [0.5 Mark] While the $-I$ effect of chlorine withdraws electron density from the ring (deactivating), the lone pairs on the chlorine atom participate in resonance with the benzene ring ($+R$ effect). This increases electron density specifically at the ortho and para positions.
  • Step 3 (Final Conclusion): [0.1 Mark] Because the resonance effect ($+R$) dominates over the inductive effect ($-I$) in terms of directing the incoming electrophile, chlorine acts as an ortho- and para-directing group.

💡 EL10 Concept Insight:

Think of the chlorine atom like a person who is naturally "stingy" ($-I$ effect pulling electrons away), but because they have extra "gifts" (lone pairs) to share, they end up pushing those gifts specifically to the corners of the room (ortho/para positions), making those spots more attractive for new guests (electrophiles) to join.
