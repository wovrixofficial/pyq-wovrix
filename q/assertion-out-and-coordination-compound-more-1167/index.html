Assertion (A) : Out of $[\text{Co}(\text{en})_3]^{3+}$ and $[\text{Co}(\text{CN})_6]^{3-}$, coordination compound $[\text{Co}(\text{en})_3]^{3+}$ is a more stable complex. Reason (R) : Ethane-1,2-diamine is a chelating ligand.
✓ Verified CBSE Marking Scheme Official Evaluation Steps & EL10 Insight

Correct Option: Assertion (A) is false, but Reason (R) is true.

Step 1 (Governing Principle & Definitions): [0.25 Mark]

The stability of a coordination complex is governed by the Chelate Effect. A chelating ligand forms a ring structure with the central metal ion, which increases the stability of the complex compared to monodentate ligands. However, the stability is also significantly influenced by the Crystal Field Splitting Energy ($\Delta_o$) and the nature of the ligand field.

Step 2 (Step-by-Step Comparative Analysis): [0.50 Mark]

  • In $[\text{Co}(\text{en})_3]^{3+}$, the ligand is ethane-1,2-diamine (en), which is a bidentate chelating ligand.
  • In $[\text{Co}(\text{CN})_6]^{3-}$, the ligand is cyanide ($\text{CN}^-$), which is a strong field monodentate ligand.
  • According to the spectrochemical series, $\text{CN}^-$ is a much stronger field ligand than $\text{en}$.
  • The stability constant ($\log K$) for $[\text{Co}(\text{CN})_6]^{3-}$ is significantly higher than that of $[\text{Co}(\text{en})_3]^{3+}$ because the strong field $\text{CN}^-$ ligand causes a much larger crystal field splitting ($\Delta_o$), leading to greater thermodynamic stability.

Step 3 (Final Concluded Answer): [0.25 Mark]

Since $[\text{Co}(\text{CN})_6]^{3-}$ is more stable than $[\text{Co}(\text{en})_3]^{3+}$, the Assertion (A) is False. The Reason (R) stating that ethane-1,2-diamine is a chelating ligand is True. Therefore, the correct choice is: (d) Assertion (A) is false, but Reason (R) is true.

💡 EL10 Concept Insight: Think of the chelate effect like a "seatbelt" that holds the ligand onto the metal, but the strength of the "glue" (the ligand field) matters more; because $\text{CN}^-$ is a much stronger "glue" than the chelating 'en', the cyanide complex stays stuck together much more tightly.
