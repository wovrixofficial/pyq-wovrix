Question: Assertion (A) : Plane and convex mirrors cannot produce real images under any circumstance. Reason (R) : A virtual image cannot serve as an object to produce a real image.

Correct Option: (d) Assertion (A) is false and Reason (R) is false.

Step 1 (Analysis of Assertion): [0.5 Mark]

The assertion states that plane and convex mirrors cannot produce real images. This is false. While it is true that these mirrors cannot form a real image of a real object, they can form a real image if the object is virtual. A virtual object is formed when converging rays are intercepted by the mirror before they meet at a point. For a mirror, the mirror formula is:

$$\frac{1}{v} + \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{f}$$

If the incident rays are converging (virtual object), $u$ is positive. If $u < |f|$, the image distance $v$ becomes positive, resulting in a real image.

Step 2 (Analysis of Reason): [0.5 Mark]

The reason states that a virtual image cannot serve as an object to produce a real image. This is also false. In optical systems involving multiple lenses or mirrors, the image formed by the first element often acts as an object for the second. If this image is virtual, it acts as a virtual object for the next element, which can then form a real image. The nature of the object (real or virtual) does not restrict the nature of the final image formed by the system.

Step 3 (Final Conclusion): [0 Mark - Integrated]

Since both the Assertion and the Reason are scientifically incorrect based on the principles of geometric optics, the correct choice is (d).

💡 EL10 Concept Insight: Think of a "virtual object" like a ghost—it's just light rays that were heading toward a point but got interrupted by a mirror. Because those rays were already converging, the mirror can easily focus them into a solid, real image on a screen!
