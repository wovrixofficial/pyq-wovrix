Chemistry The d- and f-Block Elements 2 Marks
Appeared in: 2026 (3 sets)
Atomic radii is a useful property for determining many aspects of chemistry of elements such as physical and chemical properties. The periodic table greatly assists in finding a number of trends. The transition metals belong to group $3 - 12$ and are generally characterised by partially filled d-orbitals in the free elements or their cations. The atomic radii of transition metals show a small decrease from left to right across a period, then remain nearly constant, and finally, slight increase towards the end of the series. The trend is a result of interplay between the increasing nuclear charge and the increasing shielding effect from the filling of d-orbitals. Initially the nuclear charge dominates, leading to decrease in size, but as more d-electrons are added, their shielding becomes more effective, balancing the increased nuclear charge and causing the radii to become constant. The increase in size between $3\text{d}$ and $4\text{d}$ metals is much greater than between the $4\text{d}$ and $5\text{d}$ metals. The atomic radii of $2^{\text{nd}}$ and $3^{\text{rd}}$ series are almost the same due to lanthanoid contraction. Increase in density is also observed on moving from left to right in a period. (a) Give reasons for the following : (i) The second and third series of transition metals resemble each other much more than they resemble the first series ($3\text{d}$). (ii) Transition metals show variable oxidation states.

CBSE Class 12 Chemistry: Marking Scheme Solution

(a)(i) The second and third series of transition metals resemble each other much more than they resemble the first series (3d).

  • Step 1 (Governing Principle): [1 Mark] The phenomenon responsible is Lanthanoid Contraction. As we move from the $4\text{d}$ to the $5\text{d}$ series, the filling of $4\text{f}$ orbitals occurs.
  • Step 2 (Mechanism): [0.5 Mark] The $4\text{f}$ electrons have very poor shielding effect due to their diffused shape. Consequently, the effective nuclear charge ($Z_{\text{eff}}$) increases significantly, which offsets the expected increase in atomic size due to the addition of an extra shell.
  • Step 3 (Conclusion): [0.5 Mark] As a result, the atomic radii of the $5\text{d}$ series elements are nearly identical to those of the $4\text{d}$ series elements, leading to close chemical and physical resemblance.

(a)(ii) Transition metals show variable oxidation states.

  • Step 1 (Governing Principle): [1 Mark] The variable oxidation states arise because the energy difference between the $(n-1)\text{d}$ orbitals and the $n\text{s}$ orbitals is very small.
  • Step 2 (Mechanism): [0.5 Mark] Both $(n-1)\text{d}$ and $n\text{s}$ electrons can participate in bond formation. Because these energy levels are comparable, the energy required to remove electrons from the d-subshell is easily compensated by the energy released during bond formation.
  • Step 3 (Conclusion): [0.5 Mark] This allows transition metals to lose a variable number of electrons, resulting in multiple stable oxidation states (e.g., $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ and $\text{Fe}^{3+}$).

💡 EL10 Concept Insight:

Think of Lanthanoid Contraction like a "magnetic pull" that is stronger than expected because the inner f-electrons are bad at blocking the nucleus's reach, keeping the atoms from getting bigger. For variable oxidation states, imagine the d and s electrons are like two different pockets of money that are equally easy to reach into, so the atom can choose to "spend" different amounts of electrons depending on what it's bonding with!
