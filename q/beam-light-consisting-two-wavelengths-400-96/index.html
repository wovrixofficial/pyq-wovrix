(a) A beam of light consisting of two wavelengths $400\text{ nm}$ and $600\text{ nm}$ is used to illuminate a single slit of width $1\text{ mm}$. Find the least distance of the point from the central maximum where the dark fringes due to both wavelengths coincide on the screen placed $1.5\text{ m}$ from the slit. OR (b) In a Young's double-slit experimental set-up with slit separation $0.6\text{ mm}$ a beam of light consisting of two wavelengths $440\text{ nm}$ and $660\text{ nm}$ is used to obtain interference pattern on a screen kept $1.5\text{ m}$ in front of the slits. Find the least distance of the point from the central maximum where the bright fringes due to both the wavelengths coincide.
✓ Verified CBSE Marking Scheme Official Evaluation Steps & EL10 Insight

Solution for (a): Single Slit Diffraction

Step 1 (Formula & Given Data): [0.5 Mark]

For a single slit of width $a$, the condition for the $n$-th dark fringe at a distance $y$ from the central maximum is given by:

$$y = \frac{n \lambda D}{a}$$

Given data:

  • $\lambda_1 = 400 \text{ nm} = 400 \times 10^{-9} \text{ m}$
  • $\lambda_2 = 600 \text{ nm} = 600 \times 10^{-9} \text{ m}$
  • $a = 1 \text{ mm} = 10^{-3} \text{ m}$
  • $D = 1.5 \text{ m}$

Step 2 (Step-by-Step Mathematical Working): [1.0 Mark]

For the dark fringes to coincide, the position $y$ must be the same for both wavelengths:

$$y = \frac{n_1 \lambda_1 D}{a} = \frac{n_2 \lambda_2 D}{a}$$

Canceling common terms $D$ and $a$:

$$n_1 \lambda_1 = n_2 \lambda_2 \implies \frac{n_1}{n_2} = \frac{\lambda_2}{\lambda_1} = \frac{600}{400} = \frac{3}{2}$$

For the least distance, we take the smallest integers $n_1 = 3$ and $n_2 = 2$. Substituting $n_1 = 3$ into the formula for $y$:

$$y = \frac{3 \times (400 \times 10^{-9}) \times 1.5}{10^{-3}}$$ $$y = \frac{1800 \times 10^{-9} \times 1.5}{10^{-3}} = 1800 \times 1.5 \times 10^{-6}$$

Step 3 (Final Concluded Answer): [0.5 Mark]

$$y = 2.7 \times 10^{-3} \text{ m} = 2.7 \text{ mm}$$
