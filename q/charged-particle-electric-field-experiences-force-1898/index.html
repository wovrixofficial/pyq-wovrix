Physics Moving Charges and Magnetism 1 Mark
Appeared in: 2026 (2 sets)
A charged particle +q in an electric field $\vec{E}$ experiences a force in the direction of the electric field. As a result, its kinetic energy changes. Similarly, the charged particle also experiences a force when it moves in a magnetic field $\vec{B}$. But this magnetic force is perpendicular to both velocity $\vec{v}$ of the charged particle and the magnetic field $\vec{B}$, so it cannot change the kinetic energy of the charged particle. Consider two charged particles 1 and 2 of masses m and $\frac{\text{m}}{2}$ having charges -q and +2q respectively. They are accelerated from rest through the same potential difference V and acquire kinetic energy $K_1$ and $K_2$. Then they enter in a region of uniform magnetic field $\vec{B}$ perpendicular to their velocities. (i) The ratio of their kinetic energies $\left(\frac{K_1}{K_2}\right)$ is : (A) $\frac{1}{2}$ (B) $\frac{1}{4}$ (C) 4 (D) 1
✓ Verified CBSE Marking Scheme Official Evaluation Steps & EL10 Insight

Step 1 (Formula / Governing Law & Given Data): [1 Mark]

When a charged particle of charge q is accelerated from rest through a potential difference V, the work done by the electric field is equal to the kinetic energy (K) gained by the particle. The governing formula is:

$$K = qV$$

Given data for the two particles:

  • Particle 1: Charge $q_1 = |-q| = q$, Mass $m_1 = m$
  • Particle 2: Charge $q_2 = +2q$, Mass $m_2 = \frac{m}{2}$
  • Potential difference: $V_1 = V_2 = V$

Step 2 (Step-by-Step Mathematical Working): [2 Marks]

Using the formula $K = qV$, we calculate the kinetic energies for both particles:

For particle 1:

$$K_1 = q_1 V = qV$$

For particle 2:

$$K_2 = q_2 V = (2q)V = 2qV$$

Now, we find the ratio of their kinetic energies $\frac{K_1}{K_2}$:

$$\frac{K_1}{K_2} = \frac{qV}{2qV}$$

Canceling the common terms $q$ and $V$:

$$\frac{K_1}{K_2} = \frac{1}{2}$$

Step 3 (Final Concluded Answer): [1 Mark]

The ratio of their kinetic energies is 1/2.

Comparing this with the given options:

Correct Option: (A) $\frac{1}{2}$

💡 EL10 Concept Insight: Think of the potential difference as a "voltage hill" that pushes the charges; since kinetic energy is just the charge multiplied by the voltage, a particle with twice the charge gets twice the "push," making the ratio of their energies simply the ratio of their charges.
