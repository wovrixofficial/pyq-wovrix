Mathematics Relations and Functions 4 Marks
Appeared in: 2025 (2 sets)
A class-room teacher is keen to assess the learning of her students the concept of "relations" taught to them. She writes the following five relations each defined on the set A = {1, 2, 3} : R_1 = {(2, 3), (3, 2)} R_2 = {(1, 2), (1, 3), (3, 2)} R_3 = {(1, 2), (2, 1), (1, 1)} R_4 = {(1, 1), (1, 2), (3, 3), (2, 2)} R_5 = {(1, 1), (1, 2), (3, 3), (2, 2), (2, 1), (2, 3), (3, 2)} The students are asked to answer the following questions about the above relations : (i) Identify the relation which is reflexive, transitive but not symmetric. (ii) Identify the relation which is reflexive and symmetric but not transitive. (iii) (a) Identify the relations which are symmetric but neither reflexive nor transitive. OR (iii) (b) What pairs should be added to the relation R_2 to make it an equivalence relation ?
✓ Verified CBSE Marking Scheme Official Evaluation Steps & EL10 Insight

CBSE Class 12 Mathematics: Marking Scheme Solution

Set A = {1, 2, 3}

Step 1 (Governing Definitions): [1 Mark]

  • Reflexive: $(a, a) \in R$ for all $a \in A$. (i.e., must contain $(1,1), (2,2), (3,3)$).
  • Symmetric: If $(a, b) \in R$, then $(b, a) \in R$.
  • Transitive: If $(a, b) \in R$ and $(b, c) \in R$, then $(a, c) \in R$.
  • Equivalence Relation: A relation that is reflexive, symmetric, and transitive.

Step 2 (Step-by-Step Mathematical Working): [3 Marks]

(i) Reflexive, Transitive but not Symmetric:

Check $R_4 = \{(1, 1), (1, 2), (3, 3), (2, 2)\}$.

  • Reflexive: Contains $(1,1), (2,2), (3,3)$. (Yes)
  • Symmetric: $(1,2) \in R_4$ but $(2,1) \notin R_4$. (Not Symmetric)
  • Transitive: All pairs $(a,b), (b,c)$ satisfy $(a,c) \in R_4$. (Yes)
  • Result: $R_4$

(ii) Reflexive and Symmetric but not Transitive:

Check $R_5 = \{(1, 1), (1, 2), (3, 3), (2, 2), (2, 1), (2, 3), (3, 2)\}$.

  • Reflexive: Contains $(1,1), (2,2), (3,3)$. (Yes)
  • Symmetric: $(1,2) \in R_5$ and $(2,1) \in R_5$; $(2,3) \in R_5$ and $(3,2) \in R_5$. (Yes)
  • Transitive: $(1,2) \in R_5$ and $(2,3) \in R_5$, but $(1,3) \notin R_5$. (Not Transitive)
  • Result: $R_5$

(iii) (a) Symmetric but neither Reflexive nor Transitive:

Check $R_1 = \{(2, 3), (3, 2)\}$.

  • Reflexive: $(1,1) \notin R_1$. (Not Reflexive)
  • Symmetric: $(2,3) \in R_1$ and $(3,2) \in R_1$. (Yes)
  • Transitive: $(2,3) \in R_1$ and $(3,2) \in R_1$, but $(2,2) \notin R_1$. (Not Transitive)
  • Result: $R_1$

(iii) (b) Making $R_2$ an Equivalence Relation:

$R_2 = \{(1, 2), (1, 3), (3, 2)\}$. To be an equivalence relation, it must contain all reflexive pairs, be symmetric, and be transitive.
