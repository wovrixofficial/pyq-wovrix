Appeared in: 2024 (2 sets)
OR (b) (i) Differentiate between a wavefront and a ray. (ii) State Huygen's principle and verify laws of reflection using suitable diagram. (iii) In Young's double slit experiment, the slits $S_1$ and $S_2$ are $3 \text{ mm}$ apart and the screen is placed $1.0 \text{ m}$ away from the slits. It is observed that the fourth bright fringe is at a distance of $5 \text{ mm}$ from the second dark fringe. Find the wavelength of light used.
✓ Verified CBSE Marking Scheme Official Evaluation Steps & EL10 Insight

(b) (i) Difference between Wavefront and Ray

  • Wavefront: It is the locus of all points in a medium that are oscillating in the same phase. It is a surface.
  • Ray: It is a line drawn perpendicular to the wavefront, indicating the direction of propagation of light energy.

(ii) Huygens' Principle and Verification of Laws of Reflection

Huygens' Principle:

  • Each point on a primary wavefront acts as a source of secondary wavelets, sending out spherical waves in all directions.
  • The new wavefront at any later time is the forward envelope of these secondary wavelets.

Verification of Laws of Reflection:

Consider a plane wavefront $AB$ incident on a reflecting surface $MN$. By the time point $A$ reaches $A'$, point $B$ reaches $B'$ on the surface. Using the secondary wavelets, we construct a reflected wavefront $A'B'$. In triangles $\triangle AB'A'$ and $\triangle A'B'B$, we find that the angle of incidence $i$ equals the angle of reflection $r$, verifying the law of reflection.

(iii) Numerical Solution

Step 1 (Formula / Governing Law & Given Data): [1.5 Marks]

Given: $d = 3 \text{ mm} = 3 \times 10^{-3} \text{ m}$, $D = 1.0 \text{ m}$.

Position of $n^{th}$ bright fringe: $y_{bn} = \frac{n \lambda D}{d}$

Position of $m^{th}$ dark fringe: $y_{dm} = \frac{(2m-1) \lambda D}{2d}$

Step 2 (Step-by-Step Mathematical Working): [2 Marks]

Fourth bright fringe ($n=4$): $y_{b4} = \frac{4 \lambda D}{d}$

Second dark fringe ($m=2$): $y_{d2} = \frac{(2(2)-1) \lambda D}{2d} = \frac{3 \lambda D}{2d}$

Given distance: $y_{b4} - y_{d2} = 5 \text{ mm} = 5 \times 10^{-3} \text{ m}$

$$ \frac{4 \lambda D}{d} - \frac{3 \lambda D}{2d} = 5 \times 10^{-3} $$ $$ \frac{\lambda D}{d} \left( 4 - 1.5 \right) = 5 \times 10^{-3} $$ $$ \frac{2.5 \lambda (1.0)}{3 \times 10^{-3}} = 5 \times 10^{-3} $$

Step 3 (Final Concluded Answer): [1.5 Marks]

$$ \lambda = \frac{5 \times 10^{-3} \times 3 \times 10^{-3}}{2.5} $$ $$ \lambda = 2 \times 3 \times 10^{-6} = 6 \times 10^{-6} \text{ m} = 6000 \text{ \AA} $$

The wavelength of light used is $6000 \text{ \AA}$ (or $6 \times 10^{-7} \text{ m}$).
