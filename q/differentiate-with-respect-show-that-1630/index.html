(a) Differentiate $x^x$ with respect to $x \log x$. OR (b) If $y = P \cos ux + Q \sin ux$, show that $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} + u^2y = 0$.
✓ Verified CBSE Marking Scheme Official Evaluation Steps & EL10 Insight

Solution for Part (a)

Step 1 (Formula / Governing Law & Given Data): [0.5 Mark]

Let $u = x^x$ and $v = x \log x$. We need to find the derivative of $u$ with respect to $v$, which is given by the chain rule:

$$\frac{du}{dv} = \frac{du/dx}{dv/dx}$$

Step 2 (Step-by-Step Mathematical Working): [1 Mark]

  • For $u = x^x$: Taking log on both sides, $\log u = x \log x$. Differentiating with respect to $x$: $$\frac{1}{u} \frac{du}{dx} = 1 \cdot \log x + x \cdot \frac{1}{x} = \log x + 1 \implies \frac{du}{dx} = x^x(1 + \log x)$$
  • For $v = x \log x$: Using the product rule: $$\frac{dv}{dx} = 1 \cdot \log x + x \cdot \frac{1}{x} = \log x + 1$$

Step 3 (Final Concluded Answer): [0.5 Mark]

Substituting the derivatives into the chain rule formula:

$$\frac{du}{dv} = \frac{x^x(1 + \log x)}{\log x + 1} = x^x$$

Solution for Part (b)

Step 1 (Formula / Governing Law & Given Data): [0.5 Mark]

Given $y = P \cos(ux) + Q \sin(ux)$. We apply the standard derivatives of trigonometric functions: $\frac{d}{dx}(\cos ax) = -a \sin ax$ and $\frac{d}{dx}(\sin ax) = a \cos ax$.

Step 2 (Step-by-Step Mathematical Working): [1 Mark]

  • First derivative: $$\frac{dy}{dx} = -Pu \sin(ux) + Qu \cos(ux)$$
  • Second derivative: $$\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = -Pu^2 \cos(ux) - Qu^2 \sin(ux)$$

Step 3 (Final Concluded Answer): [0.5 Mark]

Factoring out $-u^2$ from the second derivative expression:

$$\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = -u^2(P \cos(ux) + Q \sin(ux)) = -u^2y$$ $$\therefore \frac{d^2y}{dx^2} + u^2y = 0 \quad \text{(Hence Proved)}$$

💡 EL10 Concept Insight:

Think of the second derivative as the "acceleration" of a function; for sine and cosine waves, the acceleration is always just the original wave flipped upside down and scaled by the frequency squared, which is why adding them together cancels everything out to zero.
