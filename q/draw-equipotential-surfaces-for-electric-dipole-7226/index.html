Physics Electric Charges and Fields 5 Marks
Appeared in: 2024 (3 sets)
(a) (i) Draw equipotential surfaces for an electric dipole. (ii) Two point charges $q_1$ and $q_2$ are located at $\vec{r}_1$ and $\vec{r}_2$ respectively in an external electric field $\vec{E}$. Obtain an expression for the potential energy of the system. (iii) The dipole moment of a molecule is $10^{-30} \text{ Cm}$. It is placed in an electric field $\vec{E}$ of $10^5 \text{ V/m}$ such that its axis is along the electric field. The direction of $\vec{E}$ is suddenly changed by $60^\circ$ at an instant. Find the change in the potential energy of the dipole, at that instant.
✓ Verified CBSE Marking Scheme Official Evaluation Steps & EL10 Insight

(a) (i) Equipotential Surfaces for an Electric Dipole

The equipotential surfaces for an electric dipole are surfaces where the electric potential is constant. These surfaces are crowded near the charges and become more spread out as the distance from the dipole increases. They are symmetric about the axis of the dipole.

[Note: A diagram showing closed loops around each charge, with the loops becoming larger and more distorted as they encompass both charges, is required for full marks.]

(a) (ii) Potential Energy of a System of Two Charges in an External Field

Step 1 (Governing Law): [1 Mark]

The potential energy $U$ of a system of two charges $q_1$ and $q_2$ in an external field $\vec{E}$ is the sum of the work done in bringing the charges from infinity to their respective positions $\vec{r}_1$ and $\vec{r}_2$ and the work done in bringing them against their mutual electrostatic force.

Step 2 (Mathematical Derivation): [1 Mark]

  • Work done to bring $q_1$ to $\vec{r}_1$: $W_1 = q_1 V(\vec{r}_1)$
  • Work done to bring $q_2$ to $\vec{r}_2$: $W_2 = q_2 V(\vec{r}_2) + \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \frac{q_1 q_2}{|\vec{r}_1 - \vec{r}_2|}$

Step 3 (Final Expression): [1 Mark]

Total Potential Energy $U = W_1 + W_2$:

$$U = q_1 V(\vec{r}_1) + q_2 V(\vec{r}_2) + \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \frac{q_1 q_2}{|\vec{r}_1 - \vec{r}_2|}$$

(a) (iii) Numerical Calculation

Step 1 (Formula & Given Data): [1 Mark]

The potential energy of a dipole in an electric field is given by $U = -\vec{p} \cdot \vec{E} = -pE \cos\theta$.

  • $p = 10^{-30} \text{ Cm}$
  • $E = 10^5 \text{ V/m}$
  • Initial angle $\theta_1 = 0^\circ$
  • Final angle $\theta_2 = 60^\circ$

Step 2 (Mathematical Working): [0.5 Mark]

Change in potential energy $\Delta U = U_f - U_i$:

$$\Delta U = (-pE \cos 60^\circ) - (-pE \cos 0^\circ)$$ $$\Delta U = pE (\cos 0^\circ - \cos 60^\circ)$$ $$\Delta U = (10^{-30})(10^5) \left(1 - \frac{1}{2}\right)$$ $$\Delta U = 10^{-25} \times 0.5$$

Step 3 (Final Answer): [0.5 Mark]

$$\Delta U = 5 \times 10^{-26} \text{ J}$$

💡 EL10 Concept Insight: Think of the dipole like a compass needle in a magnetic field; it wants to align
