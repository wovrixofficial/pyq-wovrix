Physics Ray Optics and Optical Instruments 5 Marks
Appeared in: 2024 (3 sets)
(a) (i) Draw a ray diagram for the formation of the image of an object by a convex mirror. Hence, obtain the mirror equation. (ii) Why are multi-component lenses used for both the objective and the eyepiece in optical instruments? (iii) The magnification of a small object produced by a compound microscope is $200$. The focal length of the eyepiece is $2 \text{ cm}$ and the final image is formed at infinity. Find the magnification produced by the objective.
✓ Verified CBSE Marking Scheme Official Evaluation Steps & EL10 Insight

(a) (i) Ray Diagram and Mirror Equation

Step 1 (Ray Diagram and Geometry): [1 Mark]

Consider an object $AB$ placed in front of a convex mirror. A ray parallel to the principal axis strikes the mirror and reflects as if coming from the focus $F$. A ray directed towards the center of curvature $C$ reflects back along the same path. The intersection of these reflected rays forms a virtual, erect, and diminished image $A'B'$.

Using similar triangles $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle A'B'C$, and $\triangle ABP$ and $\triangle A'B'P$, we establish the geometric ratios based on the sign convention.

Step 2 (Mathematical Derivation): [1 Mark]

From similar triangles $\triangle A'B'F$ and $\triangle MPF$ (where $MP$ is the perpendicular from the point of incidence to the axis):

$$\frac{A'B'}{MP} = \frac{FB'}{FP} \implies \frac{A'B'}{AB} = \frac{f - v}{f}$$

From similar triangles $\triangle ABP$ and $\triangle A'B'P$:

$$\frac{A'B'}{AB} = \frac{PB'}{PB} = \frac{v}{u}$$

Equating the two:

$$\frac{f - v}{f} = \frac{v}{u} \implies 1 - \frac{v}{f} = \frac{v}{u}$$

Dividing by $v$ and rearranging:

$$\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} + \frac{1}{u}$$

Step 3 (Final Conclusion): [0.5 Mark]

The mirror equation is $\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} + \frac{1}{u}$, which relates the focal length, image distance, and object distance.

(a) (ii) Multi-component Lenses

Step 1 (Reasoning): [1 Mark]

Multi-component lenses are used to:

  • Increase the aperture and light-gathering power.
  • Minimize optical aberrations such as spherical aberration, chromatic aberration, and astigmatism, which cannot be corrected by a single lens.
  • Achieve a flatter field of view and higher image contrast.

(a) (iii) Numerical Problem

Step 1 (Given Data & Formula): [0.5 Mark]

Given: Total magnification $M = 200$, focal length of eyepiece $f_e = 2 \text{ cm}$. Final image is at infinity ($D = \infty$).

Formula for magnification of eyepiece at infinity: $m_e = \frac{D}{f_e} = \frac{25}{f_e}$ (where $D$ is the near point, $25 \text{ cm}$).

Step 2 (Mathematical Working): [0.5 Mark]

Calculate $m_e$:

$$m_e = \frac{25}{2} = 12.5$$

Total magnification $M = m_o \times m_e$:

$$200 = m_o \times 12.5$$ $$m_o = \frac{200}{12.5}$$
