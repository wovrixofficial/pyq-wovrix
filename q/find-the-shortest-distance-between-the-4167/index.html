(a) Find the shortest distance between the lines $l_1$ and $l_2$ given by : $l_1 : \vec{r} = \hat{i} + 2\hat{j} - 4\hat{k} + \lambda $4\hat{i} + 6\hat{j} + 12\hat{k}$$ and $l_2 : \vec{r} = 3\hat{i} + 3\hat{j} - 5\hat{k} + \mu $6\hat{i} + 9\hat{j} + 18\hat{k}$$ OR (b) Show that the lines $\frac{x - 1}{2} = \frac{y - 2}{3} = \frac{z - 3}{4}$ and $\frac{x - 4}{5} = \frac{y - 1}{2} = \frac{z}{1}$ intersect. Also, find their point of intersection.
✓ Verified CBSE Marking Scheme Official Evaluation Steps & EL10 Insight

Solution (a): Shortest Distance Between Two Lines

Step 1 (Formula & Identification): [1 Mark]

The given lines are in the form $\vec{r} = \vec{a_1} + \lambda \vec{b_1}$ and $\vec{r} = \vec{a_2} + \mu \vec{b_2}$.

  • $\vec{a_1} = \hat{i} + 2\hat{j} - 4\hat{k}$, $\vec{b_1} = 4\hat{i} + 6\hat{j} + 12\hat{k}$
  • $\vec{a_2} = 3\hat{i} + 3\hat{j} - 5\hat{k}$, $\vec{b_2} = 6\hat{i} + 9\hat{j} + 18\hat{k}$

Observe that $\vec{b_2} = 1.5 \vec{b_1}$, meaning the lines are parallel. The formula for the shortest distance between parallel lines is:

$$d = \frac{|\vec{b} \times (\vec{a_2} - \vec{a_1})|}{|\vec{b}|}$$

Step 2 (Mathematical Working): [2 Marks]

First, calculate $(\vec{a_2} - \vec{a_1})$:

$$\vec{a_2} - \vec{a_1} = (3-1)\hat{i} + (3-2)\hat{j} + (-5 - (-4))\hat{k} = 2\hat{i} + \hat{j} - \hat{k}$$

Now, compute the cross product $\vec{b} \times (\vec{a_2} - \vec{a_1})$ where $\vec{b} = 4\hat{i} + 6\hat{j} + 12\hat{k}$:

$$\vec{b} \times (\vec{a_2} - \vec{a_1}) = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ 4 & 6 & 12 \\ 2 & 1 & -1 \end{vmatrix}$$ $$= \hat{i}(-6 - 12) - \hat{j}(-4 - 24) + \hat{k}(4 - 12) = -18\hat{i} + 28\hat{j} - 8\hat{k}$$

Calculate magnitudes:

$$|\vec{b} \times (\vec{a_2} - \vec{a_1})| = \sqrt{(-18)^2 + 28^2 + (-8)^2} = \sqrt{324 + 784 + 64} = \sqrt{1172} = 2\sqrt{293}$$ $$|\vec{b}| = \sqrt{4^2 + 6^2 + 12^2} = \sqrt{16 + 36 + 144} = \sqrt{196} = 14$$

Step 3 (Final Concluded Answer): [1 Mark]

$$d = \frac{2\sqrt{293}}{14} = \frac{\sqrt{293}}{7} \text{ units}$$

💡 EL10 Concept Insight: Think of parallel lines like two train tracks; since they never meet, the "shortest distance" is just the perpendicular gap between them, which we find by creating a parallelogram
