In forward and reverse biased silicon p-n junction, the dominant mechanisms for the motion of charge carriers are : (A) diffusion in forward bias, drift in reverse bias (B) drift in both forward bias and in reverse bias (C) drift in forward and diffusion in reverse bias (D) diffusion in both forward and reverse bias
✓ Verified CBSE Marking Scheme Official Evaluation Steps & EL10 Insight

Question: In forward and reverse biased silicon p-n junction, the dominant mechanisms for the motion of charge carriers are:

Step 1 (Governing Principles of p-n Junction): [0.5 Mark]

  • Forward Bias: The external potential $V$ opposes the built-in potential $V_{bi}$. This reduces the depletion barrier, allowing majority charge carriers (electrons from n-side and holes from p-side) to cross the junction. This flow is driven by the concentration gradient, known as diffusion.
  • Reverse Bias: The external potential $V$ supports the built-in potential $V_{bi}$. This increases the depletion barrier, blocking majority carriers. However, the electric field $E$ across the junction sweeps minority charge carriers across, a process known as drift.

Step 2 (Analysis of Mechanisms): [0.25 Mark]

Comparing the physical processes:

  • Forward bias current $I_f \approx I_{diffusion}$
  • Reverse bias current $I_r \approx I_{drift}$

Step 3 (Final Concluded Answer): [0.25 Mark]

Based on the analysis, the correct option is (A) diffusion in forward bias, drift in reverse bias.

💡 EL10 Concept Insight: Think of the p-n junction like a gate: in forward bias, you push the gate open so the crowd (majority carriers) can walk through naturally (diffusion), but in reverse bias, the gate is locked tight, and only a few stragglers (minority carriers) get pulled through by a strong magnetic-like force (drift).
