Physics Moving Charges and Magnetism 4 Marks
Appeared in: 2026 (3 sets)
A galvanometer is used to detect or/and measure small currents in an electrical circuit. It essentially works on the fact that a current-carrying coil experiences a deflecting torque when placed in a magnetic field. This deflection in the coil can be measured and it is related to the current flowing in the coil, the number of turns in the coil, area of the coil and the magnetic field. A hair spring attached to the coil provides a counter torque and helps in measuring the deflection. A galvanometer can be converted to an ammeter or a voltmeter of desired range by using suitable resistances.

CBSE Class 12 Physics: Moving Coil Galvanometer Marking Scheme

Question Analysis: Derive the expression for the torque acting on a rectangular coil carrying current $I$ placed in a uniform magnetic field $B$, and explain how it leads to the deflection of the galvanometer.

Step 1 (Formula / Governing Law): [1 Mark]

A current-carrying loop of area $A$ with $N$ turns placed in a magnetic field $\vec{B}$ experiences a magnetic torque $\vec{\tau}$. The magnitude of this torque is given by:

$$\tau = N I A B \sin \theta$$

Where $\theta$ is the angle between the normal to the coil area and the magnetic field direction.

Step 2 (Step-by-Step Mathematical Working): [2 Marks]

  • In a radial magnetic field, the plane of the coil is always parallel to the magnetic field, making $\theta = 90^\circ$. Thus, $\sin 90^\circ = 1$.
  • The deflecting torque becomes: $$\tau_{deflecting} = N I A B$$
  • The restoring torque provided by the hair spring for a deflection $\phi$ is: $$\tau_{restoring} = k \phi$$ (where $k$ is the torsional constant of the spring).
  • At equilibrium, $\tau_{deflecting} = \tau_{restoring}$: $$N I A B = k \phi$$

Step 3 (Final Concluded Answer): [1 Mark]

The current $I$ is directly proportional to the deflection $\phi$:

$$I = \left( \frac{k}{NAB} \right) \phi \implies I \propto \phi$$

The term $\frac{k}{NAB}$ is the galvanometer constant. The final result confirms that the deflection is linear with respect to the current flowing through the coil.

💡 EL10 Concept Insight: Imagine the coil is like a tiny door hinge; when electricity flows through it, the magnetic field pushes the "door" open, but the spring acts like a door closer trying to pull it back. The point where the push of the electricity perfectly balances the pull of the spring tells us exactly how much current is flowing!
