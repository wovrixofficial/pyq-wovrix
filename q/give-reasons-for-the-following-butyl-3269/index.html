Chemistry Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers 3 Marks
Appeared in: 2025 (2 sets)
Give reasons for the following : (a) t-butyl bromide on reaction with sodium methoxide gives alkene as main product and not ether. (b) o-nitrophenol is steam volatile while p-nitrophenol is not. (c) Alcohol is less acidic than phenol.
✓ Verified CBSE Marking Scheme Official Evaluation Steps & EL10 Insight

CBSE Class 12 Chemistry: Marking Scheme Solution

(a) t-butyl bromide on reaction with sodium methoxide gives alkene as main product and not ether. [1 Mark]

  • Step 1 (Governing Principle): $t$-butyl bromide is a tertiary ($3^\circ$) alkyl halide. Sodium methoxide ($\text{CH}_3\text{ONa}$) acts as both a strong nucleophile and a strong base. [0.5 Mark]
  • Step 2 (Mechanism): Due to steric hindrance around the tertiary carbon, the nucleophilic attack ($S_N2$) is inhibited. Instead, the methoxide ion abstracts a $\beta$-hydrogen, leading to an $E2$ elimination reaction. [0.5 Mark]
  • Final Answer: The reaction yields 2-methylpropene (isobutylene) as the major product rather than the ether.

(b) o-nitrophenol is steam volatile while p-nitrophenol is not. [1 Mark]

  • Step 1 (Governing Principle): The volatility of a substance depends on the type of intermolecular forces present. [0.5 Mark]
  • Step 2 (Structural Analysis): In $o$-nitrophenol, the nitro group and hydroxyl group are close, allowing for intramolecular hydrogen bonding. In $p$-nitrophenol, the groups are far apart, leading to intermolecular hydrogen bonding between different molecules. [0.5 Mark]
  • Final Answer: Intramolecular H-bonding in $o$-nitrophenol reduces its boiling point (making it steam volatile), whereas intermolecular H-bonding in $p$-nitrophenol causes association of molecules, increasing the boiling point.

(c) Alcohol is less acidic than phenol. [1 Mark]

  • Step 1 (Governing Principle): Acidity depends on the stability of the conjugate base (alkoxide vs. phenoxide ion) formed after the loss of a proton ($H^+$). [0.5 Mark]
  • Step 2 (Comparison): The phenoxide ion is stabilized by resonance, where the negative charge is delocalized over the benzene ring. In contrast, the alkoxide ion ($R\text{O}^-$) is destabilized by the electron-donating inductive effect ($+I$ effect) of the alkyl group. [0.5 Mark]
  • Final Answer: Since the phenoxide ion is more stable than the alkoxide ion, phenol dissociates more readily, making it more acidic than alcohol.

💡 EL10 Concept Insight:

Think of it like this: (a) The bulky $t$-butyl group is like a crowded room; the nucleophile can't get in, so it just "grabs" a hydrogen from the outside instead. (b) $o$-nitrophenol is "self-contained" with its own internal bond, so it doesn't stick to its neighbors and flies off easily as steam. (c) Phenol is more acidic because it can spread out its negative charge across the whole ring (resonance), making it much happier and more stable than an alcohol's negative charge, which is stuck in one spot.
