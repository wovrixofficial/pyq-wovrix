A $5\text{ cm}$ long pencil is placed along the principal axis of a concave mirror of focal length $20\text{ cm}$ such that its nearest end is at a distance of $25\text{ cm}$ from the mirror. Calculate the length of the image of the pencil. OR In a Young's double-slit experiment, a beam of light consisting of two wavelengths $500\text{ nm}$ and $600\text{ nm}$ is used. The interference fringes are observed at a screen placed $1.8\text{ m}$ away from the plane of slits (slit separation $0.3\text{ mm}$). Calculate the least distance from the central maximum where the bright fringes due to both the wavelengths coincide.
✓ Verified CBSE Marking Scheme Official Evaluation Steps & EL10 Insight

Solution: Concave Mirror Problem

Step 1 (Formula / Governing Law & Given Data): [0.5 Mark]

The mirror formula is given by:

$$\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} + \frac{1}{u}$$

Given data (using sign convention for a concave mirror, $f = -20\text{ cm}$):

  • Focal length ($f$) = $-20\text{ cm}$
  • Distance of nearest end ($u_1$) = $-25\text{ cm}$
  • Length of pencil = $5\text{ cm}$, so distance of farthest end ($u_2$) = $-(25 + 5) = -30\text{ cm}$

Step 2 (Step-by-Step Mathematical Working): [1.0 Mark]

For the nearest end ($u_1 = -25\text{ cm}$):

$$\frac{1}{v_1} = \frac{1}{f} - \frac{1}{u_1} = \frac{1}{-20} - \frac{1}{-25} = \frac{-5 + 4}{100} = -\frac{1}{100} \implies v_1 = -100\text{ cm}$$

For the farthest end ($u_2 = -30\text{ cm}$):

$$\frac{1}{v_2} = \frac{1}{f} - \frac{1}{u_2} = \frac{1}{-20} - \frac{1}{-30} = \frac{-3 + 2}{60} = -\frac{1}{60} \implies v_2 = -60\text{ cm}$$

Step 3 (Final Concluded Answer): [0.5 Mark]

The length of the image ($L$) is the difference between the image positions:

$$L = |v_1 - v_2| = |-100 - (-60)| = |-40| = 40\text{ cm}$$

Final Answer: The length of the image of the pencil is $40\text{ cm}$.

💡 EL10 Concept Insight: Think of the mirror as a magnifying glass that stretches objects differently depending on how close they are; because the pencil is placed near the focal point, the end closer to the mirror gets pushed much further away than the far end, resulting in a significantly stretched image.
