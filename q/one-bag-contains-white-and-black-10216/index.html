One bag contains 4 white and 5 black balls. Another bag contains 6 white and 7 black balls. A ball, drawn at random, is transferred from the first bag to the second bag and then a ball is drawn at random from the second bag. Find the probability that the ball drawn is white.
✓ Verified CBSE Marking Scheme Official Evaluation Steps & EL10 Insight

Step 1 (Formula / Governing Law & Given Data): [1/4 Mark]

Let $W_1$ be the event that a white ball is transferred from Bag I to Bag II, and $B_1$ be the event that a black ball is transferred. Let $W$ be the event that the ball drawn from Bag II is white. By the Law of Total Probability:

$$P(W) = P(W_1) \cdot P(W|W_1) + P(B_1) \cdot P(W|B_1)$$

Given:

  • Bag I: 4 White, 5 Black (Total = 9)
  • Bag II: 6 White, 7 Black (Total = 13)

Step 2 (Step-by-Step Mathematical Working): [1/2 Mark]

First, calculate the probabilities of the transfer events:

$$P(W_1) = \frac{4}{9}, \quad P(B_1) = \frac{5}{9}$$

After transferring a ball, Bag II contains 14 balls. Calculate conditional probabilities for drawing a white ball from Bag II:

  • If a white ball was transferred ($W_1$): Bag II has 7 White, 7 Black. Thus, $P(W|W_1) = \frac{7}{14} = \frac{1}{2}$.
  • If a black ball was transferred ($B_1$): Bag II has 6 White, 8 Black. Thus, $P(W|B_1) = \frac{6}{14} = \frac{3}{7}$.

Substitute these into the total probability formula:

$$P(W) = \left( \frac{4}{9} \times \frac{1}{2} \right) + \left( \frac{5}{9} \times \frac{3}{7} \right)$$ $$P(W) = \frac{4}{18} + \frac{15}{63} = \frac{2}{9} + \frac{15}{63}$$ $$P(W) = \frac{14 + 15}{63} = \frac{29}{63}$$

Step 3 (Final Concluded Answer): [1/4 Mark]

The probability that the ball drawn from the second bag is white is:

$$\mathbf{P(W) = \frac{29}{63}}$$

💡 EL10 Concept Insight: Think of this like a two-path maze: you either move a white ball or a black ball first, which changes the "recipe" of the second bag. We just calculate the chance of each path happening and add them together to get the total probability!
