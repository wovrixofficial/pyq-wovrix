(a) Out of two bags, bag I contains $3$ red and $4$ white balls and bag II contains $8$ red and $6$ white balls. A die is thrown. If it shows a number less than $3$ then a ball is drawn at random from bag I, otherwise a ball is drawn at random from bag II. Find the probability that the ball drawn from one of the bags is a red ball. OR (b) The probability of simultaneous occurrence of atleast one of the two events X and Y is a. If the probability that exactly one of the events X, Y occurs is b, prove that $\text{P}(X') + \text{P}(Y') = 2 - 2a + b$.
✓ Verified CBSE Marking Scheme Official Evaluation Steps & EL10 Insight

Solution (a)

Step 1 (Formula / Governing Law & Given Data): [1 Mark]

Let $E_1$ be the event of choosing Bag I and $E_2$ be the event of choosing Bag II. Let $R$ be the event of drawing a red ball. According to the Law of Total Probability:

$$P(R) = P(E_1) \cdot P(R|E_1) + P(E_2) \cdot P(R|E_2)$$

Given data:

  • Die shows < 3 (i.e., 1 or 2): $P(E_1) = \frac{2}{6} = \frac{1}{3}$
  • Die shows ≥ 3 (i.e., 3, 4, 5, or 6): $P(E_2) = \frac{4}{6} = \frac{2}{3}$
  • Bag I (3 Red, 4 White): $P(R|E_1) = \frac{3}{7}$
  • Bag II (8 Red, 6 White): $P(R|E_2) = \frac{8}{14} = \frac{4}{7}$

Step 2 (Step-by-Step Mathematical Working): [1.5 Marks]

Substituting the values into the formula:

$$P(R) = \left( \frac{1}{3} \times \frac{3}{7} \right) + \left( \frac{2}{3} \times \frac{4}{7} \right)$$ $$P(R) = \frac{3}{21} + \frac{8}{21}$$ $$P(R) = \frac{3 + 8}{21}$$

Step 3 (Final Concluded Answer): [0.5 Mark]

$$P(R) = \frac{11}{21}$$

💡 EL10 Concept Insight: Think of this like a branching path: you first flip a coin (the die) to decide which room to enter, and then you calculate the chance of picking a red ball in that specific room; we just add up the chances from both possible paths.

Solution (b)

Step 1 (Formula / Governing Law & Given Data): [1 Mark]

Given:

  • $P(X \cup Y) = a$
  • $P(X \cup Y) - P(X \cap Y) = b$ (Probability of exactly one event)

We know $P(X \cup Y) = P(X) + P(Y) - P(X \cap Y)$.

Step 2 (Step-by-Step Mathematical Working): [1.5 Marks]

From the given equations:

$$P(X \cap Y) = P(X \cup Y) - b = a - b$$

Now, consider the expression $P(X') + P(Y')$:

$$P(X') + P(Y') = (1 - P(X)) + (1 - P(Y)) = 2 - (P(X) + P(Y))$$

Since $P(X) + P(Y) = P(X \cup Y) + P(X \cap Y)$, substitute the known values:

$$P(X) + P(Y) = a + (a - b) = 2a - b$$
