Physics Dual Nature of Radiation and Matter 3 Marks
Appeared in: 2024 (2 sets)
In photoelectric effect experiment, show the variation of (a) photocurrent with collector plate potential for a given surface for different intensities of incident radiation. Do the curves meet at any point ? If so, why ? (b) photocurrent with intensity of radiation incident on a surface keeping the frequency and plate potential fixed.
✓ Verified CBSE Marking Scheme Official Evaluation Steps & EL10 Insight

CBSE Class 12 Physics: Photoelectric Effect Marking Scheme

Step 1 (Conceptual Basis & Governing Laws): [1 Mark]

  • The photoelectric effect is governed by the principle that the number of photoelectrons emitted per second is directly proportional to the intensity of incident radiation.
  • The collector plate potential ($V$) affects the photocurrent ($I$) by accelerating or retarding the electrons. The stopping potential ($V_0$) is independent of intensity for a fixed frequency.

Step 2 (Step-by-Step Graphical Representation & Analysis): [1.5 Marks]

(a) Variation of photocurrent with collector plate potential:

For a given frequency, as intensity increases ($I_3 > I_2 > I_1$), the saturation current increases, but the stopping potential ($V_0$) remains the same.

Graph Description: The curves start from the same negative potential on the X-axis (stopping potential) and rise to different saturation current levels on the Y-axis.

Do the curves meet? Yes, they meet at the stopping potential ($V_0$) on the negative potential axis.

Reason: The stopping potential depends only on the maximum kinetic energy of the emitted photoelectrons, which is determined by the frequency of incident light ($K_{max} = h\nu - \phi_0$), not by the intensity.

Step 3 (Final Concluded Answer & Linear Relationship): [0.5 Marks]

(b) Variation of photocurrent with intensity:

For a fixed frequency and plate potential, the photocurrent ($I$) is directly proportional to the intensity ($P$) of incident radiation.

$$I \propto P$$

The graph is a straight line passing through the origin, indicating that the number of photoelectrons emitted is directly proportional to the number of incident photons (intensity).

💡 EL10 Concept Insight:

Think of intensity as the "number of bullets" (photons) hitting a target; more bullets mean more electrons knocked out, but the "speed" of the electrons only changes if you change the "type of bullet" (frequency), not how many you fire.
