Physics Dual Nature of Radiation and Matter 3 Marks
Appeared in: 2022 (3 sets)
(a) A proton and an $\alpha$ particle are moving with the same speed. Which one of them has greater value of de Broglie wavelength associated with it? Justify your answer. (b) Lights of wavelengths 430 nm, 450 nm and 660 nm are incident on a metal surface whose threshold wavelength is 600 nm. In which case/cases will photoemission take place and why? Calculate the threshold frequency of the metal surface.
✓ Verified CBSE Marking Scheme Official Evaluation Steps & EL10 Insight

Part (a): de Broglie Wavelength Comparison

Step 1 (Formula / Governing Law): [0.5 Mark]

The de Broglie wavelength $\lambda$ associated with a particle of mass $m$ moving with speed $v$ is given by:

$$\lambda = \frac{h}{mv}$$

where $h$ is Planck's constant.

Step 2 (Step-by-Step Mathematical Working): [0.5 Mark]

Given that both particles have the same speed $v$, the wavelength is inversely proportional to the mass ($ \lambda \propto \frac{1}{m} $).
Mass of $\alpha$-particle ($m_\alpha$) $\approx 4 \times$ mass of proton ($m_p$).

$$\lambda_p = \frac{h}{m_p v}, \quad \lambda_\alpha = \frac{h}{4m_p v}$$

Step 3 (Final Concluded Answer): [0.5 Mark]

Since $m_p < m_\alpha$, it follows that $\lambda_p > \lambda_\alpha$. Therefore, the proton has a greater de Broglie wavelength.

Part (b): Photoelectric Effect and Threshold Frequency

Step 1 (Formula / Governing Law): [0.5 Mark]

Photoemission occurs only if the incident wavelength $\lambda$ is less than or equal to the threshold wavelength $\lambda_0$ ($\lambda \le \lambda_0$).
The threshold frequency $\nu_0$ is given by:

$$\nu_0 = \frac{c}{\lambda_0}$$

Step 2 (Step-by-Step Mathematical Working): [0.5 Mark]

  • Given $\lambda_0 = 600 \text{ nm} = 600 \times 10^{-9} \text{ m}$.
  • Incident wavelengths: $430 \text{ nm}$, $450 \text{ nm}$, and $660 \text{ nm}$.
  • Comparing with $\lambda_0$:
    • $430 \text{ nm} < 600 \text{ nm}$ (Photoemission occurs)
    • $450 \text{ nm} < 600 \text{ nm}$ (Photoemission occurs)
    • $660 \text{ nm} > 600 \text{ nm}$ (No photoemission)
  • Calculation of $\nu_0$:
$$\nu_0 = \frac{3 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}}{600 \times 10^{-9} \text{ m}}$$ $$\nu_0 = \frac{3 \times 10^8}{6 \times 10^{-7}} = 0.5 \times 10^{15} \text{ Hz}$$

Step 3 (Final Concluded Answer): [0.5 Mark]

Photoemission will take place for 430 nm and 450 nm. The threshold frequency is $5 \times 10^{14} \text{ Hz}$.

💡 EL10 Concept Insight:

Think of the de Broglie wavelength like a "wobble"—lighter particles are easier to push around, so they have a bigger, wider "wobble" (wavelength) than heavy, sluggish particles. For light, think of the threshold wavelength as a "height requirement" for a ride
