Read the following passage and answer the questions that follow : Amines constitute an important class of organic compounds derived by replacing one or more hydrogen atoms of ammonia molecule by alkyl/aryl groups. Amines are usually formed from nitro compounds, halides, amides, etc. They exhibit hydrogen bonding which influences their physical properties. Alkyl amines are found to be stronger bases than ammonia. In aromatic amines, electron releasing and withdrawing groups, respectively increase and decrease their basic character. Reactions of amines are governed by availability of the unshared pair of electrons on nitrogen. Influence of the number of hydrogen atoms at nitrogen atom on the type of reactions and nature of products is responsible for identification and distinction between primary, secondary and tertiary amines. Reactivity of aromatic amines can be controlled by acylation process. (i) Why does aniline not give Friedel-Crafts reaction? CBSE Class 12 Chemistry: Amines - Marking Scheme Solution

(i) Why does aniline not give Friedel-Crafts reaction?

Step 1 (Governing Principle): [0.5 Mark] Aniline is a Lewis base due to the lone pair of electrons on the nitrogen atom.

Step 2 (Reaction Mechanism): [0.5 Mark] Friedel-Crafts reagents (like $\text{AlCl}_3$) are Lewis acids. Aniline reacts with $\text{AlCl}_3$ to form a salt:

$$\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 + \text{AlCl}_3 \rightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2^+\text{AlCl}_3^-$$

Step 3 (Conclusion): [0.0 Mark] The nitrogen atom acquires a positive charge, which acts as a strong deactivating group, preventing further electrophilic substitution.

💡 EL10 Concept Insight: Think of the Lewis acid as a "bully" that grabs the lone pair on the nitrogen, turning the ring "shy" and preventing it from reacting further.

(ii) Arrange in increasing order of $\text{pK}_b$ values: $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2, \text{NH}_3, \text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2, (\text{CH}_3)_3\text{N}$

Step 1 (Concept): [0.5 Mark] $\text{pK}_b$ is inversely proportional to basic strength. Stronger bases have lower $\text{pK}_b$ values.

Step 2 (Comparison): [0.5 Mark] Basic strength order: $\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 > (\text{CH}_3)_3\text{N} > \text{NH}_3 > \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2$.

Step 3 (Final Answer): [0.0 Mark] Increasing order of $\text{pK}_b$:

$$\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 < (\text{CH}_3)_3\text{N} < \text{NH}_3 < \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2$$

(iii) Distinguish between $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{NH}_2$ and $(\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2)_2\text{NH}$ by Hinsberg test

Step 1 (Reagent): [0.5 Mark] Hinsberg reagent is Benzenesulphonyl chloride ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{SO}_2\text{Cl}$).

Step 2 (Observation): [0.5 Mark] Primary amine ($\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{NH}_2$) forms N-ethylbenzenesulphonamide, which is soluble in alkali due to the acidic H attached to N. Secondary amine ($(\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2)_2\text{NH}$) forms N,N-diethylbenzenesulphonamide, which is insoluble in alkali as it lacks an acidic H.
