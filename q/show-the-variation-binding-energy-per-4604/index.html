Physics Nuclei 3 Marks
Appeared in: 2025 (3 sets)
(a) Show the variation of binding energy per nucleon with mass number. Write the significance of the binding energy curve. (b) Two nuclei with lower binding energy per nucleon form a nuclei with more binding energy per nucleon. (i) What type of nuclear reaction is it ? (ii) Whether the total mass of nuclei increases, decreases or remains unchanged ? (iii) Does the process require energy or produce energy ?
✓ Verified CBSE Marking Scheme Official Evaluation Steps & EL10 Insight

(a) Binding Energy per Nucleon Curve and Significance

Step 1 (Graph Representation): [1 Mark]

The variation of binding energy per nucleon ($BE/A$) with mass number ($A$) is represented by a graph where the $y$-axis is $BE/A$ (in MeV) and the $x$-axis is the mass number $A$. The curve rises sharply for light nuclei, reaches a maximum of about $8.8 \text{ MeV}$ for $A \approx 56$ (Iron), and then gradually decreases for heavier nuclei.

Step 2 (Significance): [0.5 Mark]

  • The curve explains the stability of nuclei: nuclei with higher $BE/A$ are more stable.
  • It explains the phenomenon of nuclear fission (heavy nuclei splitting) and nuclear fusion (light nuclei joining) to achieve a more stable state.

(b) Analysis of Nuclear Reaction

Step 1 (Identification): [0.5 Mark]

(i) Since two lighter nuclei combine to form a heavier nucleus with higher binding energy per nucleon, this process is known as Nuclear Fusion.

Step 2 (Mass Defect Analysis): [0.5 Mark]

(ii) According to the mass-energy equivalence principle, if the product nucleus is more stable (higher $BE/A$), it must have a lower mass per nucleon. Therefore, the total mass of the product nucleus is less than the sum of the masses of the initial nuclei. The difference is released as energy ($Q = \Delta m c^2$).

Step 3 (Energy Consideration): [0.5 Mark]

(iii) The process produces energy (exothermic). Because the final system is in a lower potential energy state (more stable), the excess mass is converted into kinetic energy of the products and radiation.

💡 EL10 Concept Insight: Think of binding energy like a "stability tax." When small nuclei join together (fusion) or a big nucleus breaks apart (fission) to reach the "sweet spot" of stability (like Iron), they shed some of their mass, which turns into a huge burst of energy—just like how a loose, wobbly structure becomes solid and releases energy when it snaps into a perfect, tight shape.
