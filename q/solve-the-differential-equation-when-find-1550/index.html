(a) Solve the differential equation $y e^y dx = (y^3 + 2x e^y) dy$, when $y(0) = 1$. OR (b) Find the general solution of the differential equation $(x^3 - 3xy^2) dx = (y^3 - 3x^2y) dy$.
✓ Verified CBSE Marking Scheme Official Evaluation Steps & EL10 Insight

Solution for (a): Differential Equation

Step 1 (Rearranging into Linear Form): [1 Mark]

Given the differential equation: $y e^y dx = (y^3 + 2x e^y) dy$. Rearranging to the form $\frac{dx}{dy} + P(y)x = Q(y)$:

$$\frac{dx}{dy} = \frac{y^3 + 2x e^y}{y e^y}$$ $$\frac{dx}{dy} = \frac{y^3}{y e^y} + \frac{2x e^y}{y e^y}$$ $$\frac{dx}{dy} - \left(\frac{2}{y}\right)x = y^2 e^{-y}$$

Step 2 (Integrating Factor and General Solution): [2 Marks]

Here, $P(y) = -\frac{2}{y}$ and $Q(y) = y^2 e^{-y}$.

Integrating Factor (I.F.) $= e^{\int P(y) dy} = e^{\int -\frac{2}{y} dy} = e^{-2 \ln|y|} = e^{\ln(y^{-2})} = \frac{1}{y^2}$.

The solution is given by $x \cdot (I.F.) = \int Q(y) \cdot (I.F.) dy + C$:

$$x \cdot \frac{1}{y^2} = \int (y^2 e^{-y}) \cdot \frac{1}{y^2} dy + C$$ $$\frac{x}{y^2} = \int e^{-y} dy + C$$ $$\frac{x}{y^2} = -e^{-y} + C$$

Step 3 (Applying Initial Condition and Final Answer): [1 Mark]

Given $y(0) = 1$, i.e., at $x=0, y=1$:

$$\frac{0}{1^2} = -e^{-1} + C \implies C = \frac{1}{e}$$

Substituting $C$ back into the equation:

$$\frac{x}{y^2} = -e^{-y} + \frac{1}{e} \implies x = y^2 \left( \frac{1}{e} - e^{-y} \right)$$
