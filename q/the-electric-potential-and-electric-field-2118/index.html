Physics Electric Charges and Fields 4 Marks
Appeared in: 2026 (3 sets)
The electric potential ($V$) and electric field ($E$) are closely related concepts in electrostatics. The electric field is a vector quantity that represents the force per unit charge at a given point in space, whereas electric potential is a scalar quantity that represents the potential energy per unit charge at a given point in space. Electric field and electric potential are related by the equations $E_r = \frac{-\text{d}V}{\text{d}r}$ and $\vec{E} = E_r \hat{r}$, i.e., electric field is the negative gradient of the electric potential. This means that electric field points in the direction of decreasing potential and its magnitude is the rate of change of potential with distance. The electric field is the force that drives a unit charge to move from higher potential region to lower potential region and electric potential difference between the two points determines the work done in moving a unit charge from one point to the other point. A pair of square conducting plates having sides of length $0.05\text{ m}$ are arranged parallel to each other in x-y plane. They are $0.01\text{ m}$ apart along z-axis and are connected to a $200\text{ V}$ power supply as shown in the figure. An electron enters with a speed of $3 \times 10^7\text{ ms}^{-1}$ horizontally and symmetrically in the space between the two plates. Neglect the effect of gravity on the electron.

CBSE Class 12 Physics: Marking Scheme Solution

Given Data:

  • Side of square plate ($L$) = $0.05\text{ m}$
  • Distance between plates ($d$) = $0.01\text{ m}$
  • Potential difference ($V$) = $200\text{ V}$
  • Initial velocity of electron ($u_x$) = $3 \times 10^7\text{ ms}^{-1}$
  • Charge of electron ($e$) = $1.6 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}$
  • Mass of electron ($m$) = $9.1 \times 10^{-31}\text{ kg}$

Step 1 (Formula / Governing Law & Given Data): [1 Mark]

The electric field ($E$) between two parallel plates is given by the relation between potential and distance:

$$E = \frac{V}{d}$$

The force ($F$) acting on the electron is $F = eE$, and the acceleration ($a$) is given by Newton's Second Law, $a = \frac{F}{m} = \frac{eE}{m}$.

Step 2 (Step-by-Step Mathematical Working): [2 Marks]

First, calculate the electric field intensity:

$$E = \frac{200\text{ V}}{0.01\text{ m}} = 2 \times 10^4\text{ V/m}$$

Now, calculate the acceleration of the electron along the z-axis:

$$a = \frac{eE}{m} = \frac{(1.6 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}) \times (2 \times 10^4\text{ V/m})}{9.1 \times 10^{-31}\text{ kg}}$$ $$a \approx 3.516 \times 10^{15}\text{ ms}^{-2}$$

The time ($t$) taken by the electron to cross the plates of length $L$ is:

$$t = \frac{L}{u_x} = \frac{0.05\text{ m}}{3 \times 10^7\text{ ms}^{-1}} \approx 1.67 \times 10^{-9}\text{ s}$$

The vertical deflection ($y$) is calculated using the equation of motion $y = \frac{1}{2}at^2$:

$$y = \frac{1}{2} \times (3.516 \times 10^{15}) \times (1.67 \times 10^{-9})^2$$

Step 3 (Final Concluded Answer): [1 Mark]

$$y \approx 0.0049\text{ m} \approx 4.9 \times 10^{-3}\text{ m}$$

The electron undergoes a vertical deflection of approximately $4.9\text{ mm}$ towards the positive plate.

💡 EL10 Concept Insight:

Think of the electron like a ball rolling horizontally; the electric field acts like a constant downward wind that pushes the electron toward the positive plate, causing it to curve in a parabolic path just like a ball thrown in gravity.
