(a) The figure given below shows three straight long parallel conductors (1), (2) and (3) kept in x-y plane, carrying currents 2I, I and 3I respectively as shown in figure. Find the magnitude and direction of : (i) net magnetic field at a point on conductor (1) and (ii) net magnetic force acting on unit length of conductor (1), due to conductors (2) and (3).
✓ Verified CBSE Marking Scheme Official Evaluation Steps & EL10 Insight

Solution: Magnetic Field and Force on Parallel Conductors

Assumption: Let the distance between conductor (1) and (2) be $d$, and the distance between conductor (2) and (3) be $d$. Thus, the distance between (1) and (3) is $2d$.

Step 1 (Formula / Governing Law & Given Data): [1 Mark]
  • Magnetic field due to a long straight wire: $B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r}$
  • Force per unit length between two parallel wires: $f = \frac{\mu_0 I_1 I_2}{2\pi r}$
  • Right-Hand Thumb Rule: Used to determine the direction of the magnetic field.
  • Fleming's Left-Hand Rule / Force Law: Like currents attract, opposite currents repel.
Step 2 (Step-by-Step Mathematical Working): [1.5 Marks]

(i) Net magnetic field at conductor (1):

The magnetic field at conductor (1) is produced by conductors (2) and (3). Assuming currents in (1), (2), and (3) are in the same direction (upwards):

$$B_2 = \frac{\mu_0 (I)}{2\pi d} \text{ (into the plane, } \otimes)$$ $$B_3 = \frac{\mu_0 (3I)}{2\pi (2d)} = \frac{3\mu_0 I}{4\pi d} \text{ (into the plane, } \otimes)$$

Since both fields are in the same direction:

$$B_{net} = B_2 + B_3 = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi d} + \frac{3\mu_0 I}{4\pi d} = \frac{2\mu_0 I + 3\mu_0 I}{4\pi d} = \frac{5\mu_0 I}{4\pi d}$$

(ii) Net magnetic force per unit length on conductor (1):

Force from (2) on (1): $f_{21} = \frac{\mu_0 (2I)(I)}{2\pi d} = \frac{\mu_0 I^2}{\pi d}$ (Attractive, towards right)

Force from (3) on (1): $f_{31} = \frac{\mu_0 (2I)(3I)}{2\pi (2d)} = \frac{6\mu_0 I^2}{4\pi d} = \frac{1.5\mu_0 I^2}{\pi d}$ (Attractive, towards right)

$$f_{net} = f_{21} + f_{31} = \frac{\mu_0 I^2}{\pi d} + \frac{1.5\mu_0 I^2}{\pi d} = \frac{2.5\mu_0 I^2}{\pi d}$$ Step 3 (Final Concluded Answer): [0.5 Marks]
  • Net Magnetic Field: $\frac{5\mu_0 I}{4\pi d}$ directed into the plane.
  • Net Force per unit length: $\frac{5\mu_0 I^2}{2\pi d}$ directed towards the other conductors (attractive).

💡 EL10 Concept Insight: Think of magnetic fields like invisible circular ripples around a wire; when you have multiple wires, you just add up the strength of these ripples at a specific spot, and since wires with current flowing the same way pull on each other like magnets, we just add their "pulling" forces together.
