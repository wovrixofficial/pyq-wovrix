(ii) In the figure, a ray of light is incident on a transparent liquid contained in a thin glass box at an angle of $45^\circ$ with its one face. The emergent ray passes along the face AB. Find the refractive index of the liquid.
✓ Verified CBSE Marking Scheme Official Evaluation Steps & EL10 Insight

CBSE Class 12 Physics: Solution & Marking Scheme

Step 1 (Formula / Governing Law & Given Data): [1.5 Marks]

According to Snell's Law, the relationship between the angle of incidence ($i$) and the angle of refraction ($r$) at an interface is given by:

$$n_1 \sin(i) = n_2 \sin(r)$$

Given Data:

  • Angle of incidence ($i$): The ray makes an angle of $45^\circ$ with the face, so the angle of incidence with the normal is $i = 90^\circ - 45^\circ = 45^\circ$.
  • Angle of refraction ($r$): The emergent ray passes along the face AB, meaning the angle of refraction at the second interface is $r_2 = 90^\circ$.
  • Refractive index of air ($n_1$): $1$.
  • Refractive index of liquid ($n_2$): $\mu$ (to be determined).
  • For a thin glass box, the geometry implies the angle of refraction at the first surface ($r_1$) and the angle of incidence at the second surface ($i_2$) satisfy $r_1 + i_2 = A$, where $A$ is the angle of the prism. For a rectangular box, $A = 90^\circ$. Thus, $r_1 + i_2 = 90^\circ$.

Step 2 (Step-by-Step Mathematical Working): [2.5 Marks]

Applying Snell's Law at the first interface (Air to Liquid):

$$1 \cdot \sin(45^\circ) = \mu \cdot \sin(r_1)$$ $$\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} = \mu \sin(r_1) \implies \sin(r_1) = \frac{1}{\mu\sqrt{2}} \quad \dots (1)$$

Applying Snell's Law at the second interface (Liquid to Air):

$$\mu \cdot \sin(i_2) = 1 \cdot \sin(90^\circ)$$ $$\mu \sin(i_2) = 1 \implies \sin(i_2) = \frac{1}{\mu} \quad \dots (2)$$

Since $r_1 + i_2 = 90^\circ$, we have $i_2 = 90^\circ - r_1$. Substituting this into equation (2):

$$\sin(90^\circ - r_1) = \frac{1}{\mu} \implies \cos(r_1) = \frac{1}{\mu} \quad \dots (3)$$

Using the identity $\sin^2(r_1) + \cos^2(r_1) = 1$:

$$\left(\frac{1}{\mu\sqrt{2}}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{1}{\mu}\right)^2 = 1$$ $$\frac{1}{2\mu^2} + \frac{1}{\mu^2} = 1 \implies \frac{1 + 2}{2\mu^2} = 1$$ $$\frac{3}{2\mu^2} = 1 \implies \mu^2 = \frac{3}{2} = 1.5$$

Step 3 (Final Concluded Answer): [1.0 Mark]

$$\mu = \sqrt{1.5} \approx 1.225$$

The refractive index of the liquid is $1.225$ (or $\sqrt{1.5}$).
