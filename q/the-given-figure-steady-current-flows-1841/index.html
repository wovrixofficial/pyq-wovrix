Appeared in: 2026 (3 sets)
(a) In the given figure, a steady current I flows through the circuit when points A and C are connected by a wire of negligible resistance. Find the potential difference between points B and C. (Figure shows points A, B, and C with $E_1=6\text{ V}$, $1\text{ }\Omega$ and $E_2=4\text{ V}$, $3\text{ }\Omega$). OR (b) A battery of emf $21\text{ V}$ and internal resistance $3\text{ }\Omega$ is connected to a resistor. If the current in the circuit is $3\text{ A}$, find : (i) the resistance of the resistor. (ii) the terminal voltage of the battery.
✓ Verified CBSE Marking Scheme Official Evaluation Steps & EL10 Insight

Solution (a)

Step 1 (Governing Law & Given Data): [0.5 Mark]

When points A and C are connected by a wire of negligible resistance, the circuit forms a closed loop. By applying Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (KVL) or the formula for current in a series circuit:

$$I = \frac{\sum E}{\sum R} = \frac{E_1 - E_2}{r_1 + r_2}$$

Given: $E_1 = 6\text{ V}$, $r_1 = 1\text{ }\Omega$, $E_2 = 4\text{ V}$, $r_2 = 3\text{ }\Omega$.

Step 2 (Mathematical Working): [1.0 Mark]

First, calculate the current $I$ flowing through the circuit:

$$I = \frac{6 - 4}{1 + 3} = \frac{2}{4} = 0.5\text{ A}$$

To find the potential difference between B and C ($V_{BC}$), we traverse from B to C through the branch containing $E_2$ and $r_2$:

$$V_{BC} = E_2 + I \cdot r_2$$ $$V_{BC} = 4 + (0.5 \times 3)$$ $$V_{BC} = 4 + 1.5 = 5.5\text{ V}$$

Step 3 (Final Concluded Answer): [0.5 Mark]

The potential difference between points B and C is $5.5\text{ V}$.

💡 EL10 Concept Insight: Think of the battery as a pump; when you connect the circuit, the stronger battery pushes current through the weaker one, and the voltage at the terminals is just the sum of the battery's own push and the extra "pressure" created by the current flowing through its internal resistance.

Solution (b)

Step 1 (Governing Law & Given Data): [0.5 Mark]

Using Ohm's Law for a complete circuit and the terminal voltage equation:

$$I = \frac{E}{R + r} \quad \text{and} \quad V = E - Ir$$

Given: $E = 21\text{ V}$, $r = 3\text{ }\Omega$, $I = 3\text{ A}$.

Step 2 (Mathematical Working): [1.0 Mark]

(i) Finding external resistance $R$:

$$3 = \frac{21}{R + 3} \implies R + 3 = \frac{21}{3} = 7$$ $$R = 7 - 3 = 4\text{ }\Omega$$

(ii) Finding terminal voltage $V$:

$$V = E - Ir = 21 - (3 \times 3)$$ $$V = 21 - 9 = 12\text{ V}$$

Step 3 (Final Concluded Answer): [0.5 Mark]

(i) The resistance of the resistor is $4\text{ }\Omega$. (ii) The terminal voltage is $12\text{ V}$.
