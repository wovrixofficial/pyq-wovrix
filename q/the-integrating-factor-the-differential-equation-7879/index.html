The integrating factor of the differential equation $(1 - y^2) \frac{dx}{dy} + yx = ay$, $(-1 < y < 1)$ is (A) $\frac{1}{y^2 - 1}$ (B) $\frac{1}{\sqrt{y^2 - 1}}$ (C) $\frac{1}{1 - y^2}$ (D) $\frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - y^2}}$
✓ Verified CBSE Marking Scheme Official Evaluation Steps & EL10 Insight

Step 1 (Formula / Governing Law & Given Data): [0.5 Mark]

The given differential equation is of the form:

$$(1 - y^2) \frac{dx}{dy} + yx = ay$$

To find the Integrating Factor (I.F.), we first convert this into the standard linear form: $\frac{dx}{dy} + P(y)x = Q(y)$. Dividing the entire equation by $(1 - y^2)$:

$$\frac{dx}{dy} + \left( \frac{y}{1 - y^2} \right)x = \frac{ay}{1 - y^2}$$

Here, $P(y) = \frac{y}{1 - y^2}$.

Step 2 (Step-by-Step Mathematical Working): [0.25 Mark]

The formula for the Integrating Factor is $I.F. = e^{\int P(y) dy}$.

$$I.F. = e^{\int \frac{y}{1 - y^2} dy}$$

Let $1 - y^2 = t$, then $-2y \, dy = dt$, which implies $y \, dy = -\frac{1}{2} dt$.

$$I.F. = e^{-\frac{1}{2} \int \frac{1}{t} dt} = e^{-\frac{1}{2} \ln|t|} = e^{\ln|t|^{-1/2}} = t^{-1/2}$$ $$I.F. = \frac{1}{\sqrt{t}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - y^2}}$$

Step 3 (Final Concluded Answer): [0.25 Mark]

Comparing the result with the given options, the correct choice is (D).

Final Answer: $\frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - y^2}}$

💡 EL10 Concept Insight: Think of the Integrating Factor as a "magic multiplier" that turns a messy differential equation into a simple product rule derivative, making it easy to solve by just integrating both sides.
