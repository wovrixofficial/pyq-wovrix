The intermediate image formed in a compound microscope is : (A) real, inverted and magnified (B) real, erect and magnified (C) virtual, erect and magnified (D) virtual, inverted and magnified
✓ Verified CBSE Marking Scheme Official Evaluation Steps & EL10 Insight

Question: The intermediate image formed in a compound microscope is:

Step 1 (Governing Principle & Optical Setup): [0.5 Mark]

In a compound microscope, the objective lens ($L_o$) is a convex lens of short focal length ($f_o$). The object is placed just beyond the focus ($f_o < u_o < 2f_o$). According to the lens formula:

$$\frac{1}{f_o} = \frac{1}{v_o} - \frac{1}{u_o}$$

The objective lens forms the intermediate image ($I_1$) of the object. Since the object is placed outside the focal point of the objective lens, the lens acts as a real image projector.

Step 2 (Step-by-Step Optical Analysis): [0.25 Mark]

  • Nature: Because the light rays actually converge at the image position, the image is real.
  • Orientation: For a single convex lens, a real image formed by a real object is always inverted with respect to the object.
  • Size: Since the object is placed close to the focus ($u_o \approx f_o$), the magnification $m_o = \frac{v_o}{u_o}$ results in a magnified image.

Step 3 (Final Concluded Answer): [0.25 Mark]

Based on the optical properties derived above, the intermediate image is real, inverted, and magnified.

Correct Option: (A)

💡 EL10 Concept Insight: Think of the objective lens like a projector lens; it takes the tiny object and flips it upside down while stretching it out to make it bigger, creating a "real" image that you could actually catch on a screen if you placed one inside the microscope tube.
