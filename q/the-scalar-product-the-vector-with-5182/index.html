Mathematics Vectors 4 Marks
Appeared in: 2025 (3 sets)
(a) The scalar product of the vector $\vec{a} = \hat{i} - \hat{j} + 2\hat{k}$ with a unit vector along sum of vectors $\vec{b} = 2\hat{i} - 4\hat{j} + 5\hat{k}$ and $\vec{c} = \lambda\hat{i} - 2\hat{j} - 3\hat{k}$ is equal to $1$. Find the value of $\lambda$. OR (b) Find the shortest distance between the lines : $$\vec{r} = $2\hat{i} - \hat{j} + 3\hat{k}$ + \lambda$\hat{i} - 2\hat{j} + 3\hat{k}$$$ $$\vec{r} = $\hat{i} + 4\hat{k}$ + \mu$3\hat{i} - 6\hat{j} + 9\hat{k}$$$
✓ Verified CBSE Marking Scheme Official Evaluation Steps & EL10 Insight

Solution (a)

Step 1 (Formula & Given Data): [1 Mark]

Given vectors: $\vec{a} = \hat{i} - \hat{j} + 2\hat{k}$, $\vec{b} = 2\hat{i} - 4\hat{j} + 5\hat{k}$, and $\vec{c} = \lambda\hat{i} - 2\hat{j} - 3\hat{k}$.

Let $\vec{d} = \vec{b} + \vec{c}$. The unit vector along $\vec{d}$ is $\hat{d} = \frac{\vec{d}}{|\vec{d}|}$. The condition given is $\vec{a} \cdot \hat{d} = 1$.

Step 2 (Step-by-Step Mathematical Working): [2 Marks]

First, calculate the sum vector $\vec{d}$:

$$\vec{d} = (2+\lambda)\hat{i} + (-4-2)\hat{j} + (5-3)\hat{k} = (2+\lambda)\hat{i} - 6\hat{j} + 2\hat{k}$$

The scalar product is:

$$\vec{a} \cdot \frac{\vec{d}}{|\vec{d}|} = 1 \implies \vec{a} \cdot \vec{d} = |\vec{d}|$$

Calculate $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{d}$:

$$\vec{a} \cdot \vec{d} = (1)(2+\lambda) + (-1)(-6) + (2)(2) = 2 + \lambda + 6 + 4 = \lambda + 12$$

Calculate $|\vec{d}| = \sqrt{(2+\lambda)^2 + (-6)^2 + (2)^2} = \sqrt{\lambda^2 + 4\lambda + 4 + 36 + 4} = \sqrt{\lambda^2 + 4\lambda + 44}$

Equating the two:

$$\lambda + 12 = \sqrt{\lambda^2 + 4\lambda + 44}$$

Squaring both sides:

$$\lambda^2 + 24\lambda + 144 = \lambda^2 + 4\lambda + 44$$ $$20\lambda = -100$$

Step 3 (Final Concluded Answer): [1 Mark]

$$\lambda = -5$$

💡 EL10 Concept Insight: Think of the dot product as a way to measure how much one vector "pushes" in the direction of another; by turning the sum of two vectors into a unit vector (length of 1), we are simply finding the exact direction where the "push" of vector $\vec{a}$ equals exactly 1 unit.

Solution (b)

Step 1 (Formula & Given Data): [1 Mark]

The shortest distance $d$ between two lines $\vec{r} = \vec{a_1} + \lambda\vec{b_1}$ and $\vec{r} = \vec{a_2} + \mu\vec{b_2}$ is given by:

$$d = \left| \frac{(\vec{a_2} - \vec{a_1}) \cdot (\vec{b_1} \times \vec{b_2})}{|\vec{b_1} \times \vec{b_2}|} \right|$$
