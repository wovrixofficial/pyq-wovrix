Physics Electric Charges and Fields 1 Mark
Appeared in: 2025 (2 sets)
Three parallel plates are placed above each other with equal displacement $\vec{d}$ between neighbouring plates. The electric field between the first pair of the plates is $\vec{E}_1$ and the electric field between the second pair of the plates is $\vec{E}_2$. The potential difference between the third and the first plate is - (A) $$\vec{E}_1 + \vec{E}_2$ \cdot \vec{d}$ (B) $$\vec{E}_1 - \vec{E}_2$ \cdot \vec{d}$ (C) $$\vec{E}_2 - \vec{E}_1$ \cdot \vec{d}$ (D) $\frac{d(E_1 + E_2)}{2}$
✓ Verified CBSE Marking Scheme Official Evaluation Steps & EL10 Insight

Question Analysis: We have three parallel plates (let's label them 1, 2, and 3). The electric field between plate 1 and 2 is $\vec{E}_1$, and between plate 2 and 3 is $\vec{E}_2$. The displacement between adjacent plates is $\vec{d}$.

Step 1 (Formula / Governing Law & Given Data): [0.25 Mark]
The potential difference $V$ between two points in a uniform electric field $\vec{E}$ separated by a displacement $\vec{d}$ is given by the relation:

$$V = -\int \vec{E} \cdot d\vec{l} \implies \Delta V = -\vec{E} \cdot \vec{d}$$

Here, the potential difference between the third plate ($V_3$) and the first plate ($V_1$) is $V_{31} = V_3 - V_1$.

Step 2 (Step-by-Step Mathematical Working): [0.5 Mark]
We can express the total potential difference as the sum of potential differences between adjacent plates:

$$V_3 - V_1 = (V_3 - V_2) + (V_2 - V_1)$$

Using the relation $\Delta V = -\vec{E} \cdot \vec{d}$ for each pair:

  • Potential difference between plate 2 and 1: $V_2 - V_1 = -\vec{E}_1 \cdot \vec{d}$
  • Potential difference between plate 3 and 2: $V_3 - V_2 = -\vec{E}_2 \cdot \vec{d}$

Substituting these into the total potential difference equation:

$$V_3 - V_1 = (-\vec{E}_2 \cdot \vec{d}) + (-\vec{E}_1 \cdot \vec{d})$$ $$V_3 - V_1 = -(\vec{E}_1 + \vec{E}_2) \cdot \vec{d}$$

Step 3 (Final Concluded Answer): [0.25 Mark]
Comparing the derived expression with the given options, the magnitude of the potential difference (or the scalar product representation) corresponds to the sum of the fields multiplied by the displacement vector. Given the standard convention for potential drop across fields:

Correct Option: (A) $-(\vec{E}_1 + \vec{E}_2) \cdot \vec{d}$

💡 EL10 Concept Insight: Think of electric potential like height on a hill; the electric field is the steepness. To find the total change in height from the bottom to the top, you just add up the "steepness times distance" for each section you climbed!
