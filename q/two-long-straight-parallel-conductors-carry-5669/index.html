(a) Two long, straight, parallel conductors carry steady currents in opposite directions. Explain the nature of the force of interaction between them. Obtain an expression for the magnitude of the force between the two conductors. Hence define one ampere. OR (b) Obtain an expression for the torque $\vec{\tau}$ acting on a current carrying loop in a uniform magnetic field $\vec{B}$. Draw the necessary diagram.
✓ Verified CBSE Marking Scheme Official Evaluation Steps & EL10 Insight

Solution for Part (a): Force between two parallel current-carrying conductors

Step 1 (Governing Law & Setup): [1 Mark]

Consider two long, straight, parallel conductors $A$ and $B$ separated by a distance $d$, carrying currents $I_1$ and $I_2$ in opposite directions. According to the Right-Hand Thumb Rule, the magnetic field $B_1$ produced by conductor $A$ at the position of conductor $B$ is:

$$B_1 = \frac{\mu_0 I_1}{2\pi d}$$

The direction of $B_1$ is perpendicular to the plane of the conductors (directed into the page). By Fleming’s Left-Hand Rule, the force on a segment of length $L$ of conductor $B$ is repulsive.

Step 2 (Mathematical Derivation): [1.5 Marks]

The magnetic force $F$ on a current-carrying conductor of length $L$ in a magnetic field $B$ is given by $F = I_2 L B_1 \sin(90^\circ)$. Substituting the value of $B_1$:

$$F = I_2 L \left( \frac{\mu_0 I_1}{2\pi d} \right)$$

The force per unit length ($f = F/L$) is:

$$f = \frac{\mu_0 I_1 I_2}{2\pi d}$$

Since the currents are in opposite directions, the force is repulsive.

Step 3 (Definition of One Ampere): [0.5 Mark]

One Ampere is defined as that constant current which, when maintained in each of the two infinitely long, straight, parallel conductors of negligible cross-section placed 1 meter apart in vacuum, produces a force of $2 \times 10^{-7} \, \text{N/m}$ between them.

💡 EL10 Concept Insight

Think of the magnetic fields around the wires like invisible gears; when currents flow in opposite directions, the magnetic fields "push" against each other in the space between the wires, forcing them apart just like two magnets with the same poles facing each other.

Solution for Part (b): Torque on a current loop in a uniform magnetic field

Step 1 (Governing Law & Diagram Description): [1 Mark]

Consider a rectangular loop $PQRS$ of sides $a$ and $b$ carrying current $I$ in a uniform magnetic field $\vec{B}$. The torque $\vec{\tau}$ is given by the cross product of the magnetic moment $\vec{m}$ and the magnetic field $\vec{B}$:

$$\vec{\tau} = \vec{m} \times \vec{B}$$
