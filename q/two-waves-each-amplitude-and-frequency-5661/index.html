(a) Two waves, each of amplitude 'a' and frequency '$\omega$' emanating from two coherent sources of light superpose at a point. If the phase difference between the two waves is $\phi$, obtain an expression for the resultant intensity at that point.
✓ Verified CBSE Marking Scheme Official Evaluation Steps & EL10 Insight

Step 1 (Formula / Governing Law & Given Data): [0.5 Mark]

Let the two coherent waves be represented by their displacement equations:

$$y_1 = a \sin(\omega t)$$ $$y_2 = a \sin(\omega t + \phi)$$

According to the Principle of Superposition, the resultant displacement $y$ is given by $y = y_1 + y_2$. The intensity $I$ of a wave is directly proportional to the square of its amplitude ($I \propto A^2$).

Step 2 (Step-by-Step Mathematical Working): [1.0 Mark]

Using the superposition principle:

$$y = a \sin(\omega t) + a \sin(\omega t + \phi)$$

Applying the trigonometric identity $\sin C + \sin D = 2 \sin\left(\frac{C+D}{2}\right) \cos\left(\frac{C-D}{2}\right)$:

$$y = 2a \sin\left(\frac{\omega t + \omega t + \phi}{2}\right) \cos\left(\frac{\omega t - (\omega t + \phi)}{2}\right)$$ $$y = 2a \sin\left(\omega t + \frac{\phi}{2}\right) \cos\left(-\frac{\phi}{2}\right)$$

Since $\cos(-\theta) = \cos(\theta)$, we have:

$$y = [2a \cos(\phi/2)] \sin(\omega t + \phi/2)$$

The resultant amplitude $A$ is $2a \cos(\phi/2)$. Since $I \propto A^2$, let $I_0$ be the intensity of each individual wave ($I_0 \propto a^2$), then:

$$I \propto (2a \cos(\phi/2))^2 = 4a^2 \cos^2(\phi/2)$$

Step 3 (Final Concluded Answer): [0.5 Mark]

Substituting $I_0$ for $a^2$, the expression for the resultant intensity is:

$$I = 4I_0 \cos^2\left(\frac{\phi}{2}\right)$$

💡 EL10 Concept Insight: Think of this like two people pushing a swing; if they push at the exact same time (phase difference of 0), the swing goes higher, but if they push at different times, they might cancel each other's effort out, creating a pattern of bright and dark spots.
