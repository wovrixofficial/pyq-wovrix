(a) Verify whether the function f defined by $f(x) = \begin{cases} x \sin\left$ \frac{1}{x} \right$, & x \neq 0 \\ 0, & x = 0 \end{cases}$ is continuous at $x = 0$ or not. OR (b) Check for differentiability of the function f defined by $f(x) = |x - 5|$, at the point $x = 5$.
✓ Verified CBSE Marking Scheme Official Evaluation Steps & EL10 Insight

Solution (a): Continuity of $f(x)$ at $x = 0$

Step 1 (Governing Law & Given Data): [0.5 Mark]

A function $f(x)$ is continuous at $x = a$ if and only if:

$$\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = f(a)$$

Given: $f(x) = x \sin\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)$ for $x \neq 0$ and $f(0) = 0$.

Step 2 (Step-by-Step Mathematical Working): [1.0 Mark]

We evaluate the limit as $x \to 0$:

$$\lim_{x \to 0} f(x) = \lim_{x \to 0} \left[ x \sin\left(\frac{1}{x}\right) \right]$$

We know that the range of the sine function is $[-1, 1]$, i.e.,

$$-1 \leq \sin\left(\frac{1}{x}\right) \leq 1$$

Multiplying the inequality by $x$ (where $x > 0$):

$$-|x| \leq x \sin\left(\frac{1}{x}\right) \leq |x|$$

Applying the Sandwich Theorem (Squeeze Theorem):

$$\lim_{x \to 0} (-|x|) = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad \lim_{x \to 0} (|x|) = 0$$

Therefore, $\lim_{x \to 0} x \sin\left(\frac{1}{x}\right) = 0$.

Step 3 (Final Concluded Answer): [0.5 Mark]

Since $\lim_{x \to 0} f(x) = 0$ and $f(0) = 0$, the condition $\lim_{x \to 0} f(x) = f(0)$ is satisfied.

Conclusion: The function $f(x)$ is continuous at $x = 0$.
