(a) (i) Why do $\text{Zr}$ and $\text{Hf}$ exhibit similar properties ? (ii) "$E^{\circ}$ for $\text{Mn}^{3+}/\text{Mn}^{2+}$ couple is more positive than that for $\text{Fe}^{3+}/\text{Fe}^{2+}$." Justify the statement. [Atomic number : $\text{Mn} = 25$, $\text{Fe} = 26$] OR (b) (i) Which element in $3\text{d}$ series of transition elements does not exhibit variable oxidation states ? Give reason. (ii) Explain why $\text{Cu}^{+}$ is colourless in aqueous solution, whereas $\text{Cu}^{2+}$ is coloured.
✓ Verified CBSE Marking Scheme Official Evaluation Steps & EL10 Insight

Solution (a)

(i) Why do $\text{Zr}$ and $\text{Hf}$ exhibit similar properties? [1 Mark]

  • Step 1 (Concept): The similarity arises due to the Lanthanoid Contraction. [0.5 Mark]
  • Step 2 (Reasoning): Due to the imperfect shielding of the $4\text{f}$ electrons, the atomic radii of $\text{Zr}$ ($160\text{ pm}$) and $\text{Hf}$ ($159\text{ pm}$) are almost identical. This similarity in size leads to similar chemical properties. [0.5 Mark]

(ii) "$E^{\circ}$ for $\text{Mn}^{3+}/\text{Mn}^{2+}$ couple is more positive than that for $\text{Fe}^{3+}/\text{Fe}^{2+}$." Justify. [1 Mark]

  • Step 1 (Electronic Configuration): $$\text{Mn}^{2+} : [\text{Ar}] 3\text{d}^5 \text{ (stable half-filled configuration)}$$ $$\text{Fe}^{3+} : [\text{Ar}] 3\text{d}^5 \text{ (stable half-filled configuration)}$$ [0.5 Mark]
  • Step 2 (Justification): The reduction of $\text{Mn}^{3+}$ to $\text{Mn}^{2+}$ results in the stable $3\text{d}^5$ configuration, making the process highly favorable (more positive $E^{\circ}$). Conversely, $\text{Fe}^{3+}$ is already stable ($3\text{d}^5$); reducing it to $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ ($3\text{d}^6$) is less favorable, resulting in a less positive $E^{\circ}$ value. [0.5 Mark]

💡 EL10 Concept Insight: Think of $\text{Mn}^{2+}$ as having a "perfectly balanced" half-filled shell that it really wants to reach, so it grabs electrons very easily, making its reduction potential high!

Solution (b)

(i) Which element in $3\text{d}$ series does not exhibit variable oxidation states? [1 Mark]

  • Step 1 (Identification): Scandium ($\text{Sc}$, $Z=21$). [0.5 Mark]
  • Step 2 (Reasoning): The electronic configuration of $\text{Sc}$ is $[\text{Ar}] 3\text{d}^1 4\text{s}^2$. Upon losing three electrons, it achieves the stable noble gas configuration ($[\text{Ar}]$). It does not have enough energy to remove further electrons from the stable inner shell. [0.5 Mark]

(ii) Explain why $\text{Cu}^{+}$ is colourless, whereas $\text{Cu}^{2+}$ is coloured. [1 Mark]

  • Step 1 (Electronic Configuration): $$\text{Cu}^{+} (Z=29) : [\text{Ar}] 3\text{d}^{10}$$ $$\text{Cu}^{2+} (Z=29) : [\text{Ar}] 3\text{d}^9$$ [0.5 Mark]
  • Step 2 (Deduction): $\text{Cu}^{+}$ has a completely filled $3\text{d}$ subshell; hence, no $d-d$ transition is possible, making it colourless. $\text{Cu}^{2+}$ has an incomplete $3\text{d}$ subshell ($3\text{d}^9$), allowing for $d-d$ transitions by absorbing light from the visible region,
