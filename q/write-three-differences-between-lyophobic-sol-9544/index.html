Chemistry Unclassified 3 Marks
Appeared in: 2022 (3 sets)
(a) Write three differences between Lyophobic sol and Lyophilic sol. OR (b) (i) Write the dispersed phase and dispersion medium of 'milk'. (ii) What is the cause of Brownian movement in colloidal particles ? (iii) Why does physisorption decrease with increase in temperature ?
✓ Verified CBSE Marking Scheme Official Evaluation Steps & EL10 Insight

Solution (a): Differences between Lyophilic and Lyophobic Sols

Step 1 (Definition and Basis of Classification): [1 Mark]

The classification is based on the affinity between the dispersed phase and the dispersion medium. Lyophilic sols are "liquid-loving," while Lyophobic sols are "liquid-hating."

Step 2 (Comparative Analysis): [1 Mark]

  • Lyophilic Sols: These are reversible sols. They are self-stabilized due to strong forces of interaction between the dispersed phase and the dispersion medium.
  • Lyophobic Sols: These are irreversible sols. They are unstable and require stabilizing agents (electrolytes) to prevent coagulation.

Step 3 (Final Tabular Distinction): [1 Mark]

Property Lyophilic Sol Lyophobic Sol
Preparation Directly formed by mixing with the medium. Prepared by special methods only.
Stability Quite stable. Less stable; easily coagulated.
Viscosity Much higher than that of the medium. Same as that of the medium.

Solution (b): Conceptual Questions

(i) Dispersed Phase and Dispersion Medium of Milk: [1 Mark]

Step 1 (Identification): Milk is an emulsion of liquid in liquid.

Step 2 (Classification): Dispersed Phase = Fat; Dispersion Medium = Water.

(ii) Cause of Brownian Movement: [1 Mark]

Step 1 (Governing Principle): Brownian movement is the continuous zig-zag motion of colloidal particles.

Step 2 (Mechanism): It is caused by the unbalanced, continuous bombardment of the colloidal particles by the molecules of the dispersion medium, which are in constant thermal motion.

(iii) Temperature Effect on Physisorption: [1 Mark]

Step 1 (Governing Law): Physisorption is an exothermic process governed by the equilibrium: $$\text{Gas} + \text{Solid} \rightleftharpoons \text{Gas-Solid Adsorbate} + \text{Heat}$$

Step 2 (Le Chatelier's Principle): According to Le Chatelier's principle, increasing the temperature shifts the equilibrium in the backward direction (endothermic direction) to absorb the excess heat, thereby decreasing the amount of gas adsorbed.

💡 EL10 Concept Insight:

Think of physisorption like a weak magnet sticking to a fridge; if you heat it up, the molecules start vibrating so wildly that they "shake off" the surface, which is why adsorption drops as things get hotter!
