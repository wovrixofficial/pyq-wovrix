(ii) In Young's double slit experiment, find the ratio of intensities at two points on a screen when waves emanating from two slits reaching these points have path differences (i) $\frac{\lambda}{6}$ and (ii) $\frac{\lambda}{12}$.
✓ Verified CBSE Marking Scheme Official Evaluation Steps & EL10 Insight

CBSE Class 12 Physics: Solution for Young's Double Slit Experiment

Step 1 (Formula / Governing Law & Given Data): [1.0 Mark]

The intensity $I$ at any point on the screen in Young's Double Slit Experiment (YDSE) is given by the relation:

$$I = I_{max} \cos^2\left(\frac{\phi}{2}\right)$$

Where $\phi$ is the phase difference between the two waves. The relationship between phase difference ($\phi$) and path difference ($\Delta x$) is:

$$\phi = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda} \cdot \Delta x$$

Given: $I_1$ is intensity at $\Delta x_1 = \frac{\lambda}{6}$ and $I_2$ is intensity at $\Delta x_2 = \frac{\lambda}{12}$.

Step 2 (Step-by-Step Mathematical Working): [3.0 Marks]

  • For path difference $\Delta x_1 = \frac{\lambda}{6}$: $$\phi_1 = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda} \cdot \frac{\lambda}{6} = \frac{\pi}{3}$$ $$I_1 = I_{max} \cos^2\left(\frac{\pi/3}{2}\right) = I_{max} \cos^2\left(\frac{\pi}{6}\right)$$ $$I_1 = I_{max} \left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{3}{4} I_{max}$$
  • For path difference $\Delta x_2 = \frac{\lambda}{12}$: $$\phi_2 = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda} \cdot \frac{\lambda}{12} = \frac{\pi}{6}$$ $$I_2 = I_{max} \cos^2\left(\frac{\pi/6}{2}\right) = I_{max} \cos^2\left(\frac{\pi}{12}\right)$$ Using the identity $\cos^2\theta = \frac{1 + \cos(2\theta)}{2}$: $$I_2 = I_{max} \left( \frac{1 + \cos(\pi/6)}{2} \right) = I_{max} \left( \frac{1 + \sqrt{3}/2}{2} \right) = I_{max} \left( \frac{2 + \sqrt{3}}{4} \right)$$

Step 3 (Final Concluded Answer): [1.0 Mark]

The ratio of intensities $\frac{I_1}{I_2}$ is:

$$\frac{I_1}{I_2} = \frac{\frac{3}{4} I_{max}}{\left( \frac{2 + \sqrt{3}}{4} \right) I_{max}} = \frac{3}{2 + \sqrt{3}}$$

Rationalizing the denominator (multiplying by $2 - \sqrt{3}$):

$$\frac{I_1}{I_2} = \frac{3(2 - \sqrt{3})}{4 - 3} = 3(2 - \sqrt{3}) = 6 - 3\sqrt{3}$$

Final Answer: The ratio of intensities is $3 : (2 + \sqrt{3})$ or approximately $0.804 : 1$.

💡 EL10 Concept Insight: Think of light waves like two people walking on a track; if one person takes a slightly longer path (path difference), they fall out of sync (phase difference), which makes them bump
